HEIC to JPG: Complete Guide

Convert iPhone HEIC photos to JPG in your browser. Why HEIC exists, how decoding works, quality, and what to do with Live Photos.

If you own an iPhone, most of your photos are HEIC files, whether you asked for them or not. They look perfect on your phone and refuse to open on half the computers and websites you meet.

Converting HEIC to JPG is the fix. JPG opens everywhere, from a ten-year-old Windows laptop to a government upload form, and the conversion takes seconds.

This guide explains what HEIC is, why Apple uses it, and how to convert it cleanly without losing the photos you care about.

What Is HEIC?

HEIC is a photo format based on the HEIF container, which stores images with modern compression. Apple made it the default camera format on iPhones starting with iOS 11.

The format stores the same quality as JPG in roughly half the space. That efficiency is why your iPhone holds twice as many photos as it did in the JPG era.

HEIC files can also hold extra data in the same file, such as depth information, burst sequences, and the short video clip that makes a Live Photo live.

The catch is compatibility. Outside the Apple world, many programs, websites, and printers simply do not recognise HEIC, and they reject the file without explanation.

Why Do iPhones Save Photos as HEIC?

Apple switched to HEIC to save storage space. Phones take enormous photos now, and halving each file means room for twice as many memories.

The format also supports features JPG cannot, such as 16-bit colour, transparency, and multiple images in one file. Live Photos and portrait depth maps depend on it.

Apple devices handle HEIC natively, so iPhone users rarely notice the format at all. The problem only appears when a photo leaves the Apple ecosystem.

You can change the camera setting to save as JPG instead, but most people never do. The default is HEIC, and the default is what most photos become.

Why Do Windows PCs Reject HEIC Files?

Windows does not include a HEIC decoder by default. The Photos app may offer to download a codec, but many users never see that prompt, and the file simply fails to open.

Older software is worse. Office versions, email clients, and upload forms built before HEIC existed have no idea what the file is, and they reject it outright.

Websites are mixed. Some modern sites accept HEIC, but most upload forms list only JPG and PNG, and a HEIC file bounces straight back.

Converting to JPG removes the problem entirely. JPG has worked everywhere for thirty years, and no system built in that time rejects it.

How Does HEIC Decoding Work in a Browser?

Browsers cannot decode HEIC natively, so the converter loads a small WebAssembly decoder inside your browser tab. That decoder reads the HEIC container and renders the photo.

The decoding happens on your device, using your processor. A standard 12-megapixel photo decodes in a second or two, while a 48-megapixel ProRAW file takes a little longer.

Once decoded, the photo is drawn onto a canvas and exported as a standard JPG, PNG, or WebP. The result is an ordinary image file that any program can open.

No server is involved at any point. Your photos never leave your device, which matters when the pictures include family, travel, or identity documents.

Why 90 Percent Is the Default Quality

The HEIC to JPG converter defaults to 90 percent JPG quality. That setting is deliberate, since it preserves the detail that makes iPhone photos look good.

HEIC files are efficient, so converting them at a low JPG quality would throw away detail that the original worked hard to keep. A 90 percent JPG keeps the photo looking like itself.

Lower settings save space, but the savings are rarely worth it for photos you care about. A drop from 90 to 80 percent halves the file size yet visibly softens fine detail.

For photos going to a form with a size limit, lower the quality only as far as needed. Start at 90, check the size, and step down gradually.

Why Converted JPGs Are Bigger Than HEICs

A converted JPG is almost always larger than the original HEIC, often by double. That surprises people, but it is normal and expected.

HEIC is a more efficient format than JPG. Moving from a modern format to an older one costs space, because JPG simply cannot pack the same detail into fewer bytes.

The JPG is not worse than the HEIC. It holds the same picture, just stored less efficiently. The larger size is the price of universal compatibility.

If the larger size is a problem, use the compressor after converting. A JPG at 85 percent quality is far smaller than one at 95 percent, and still looks clean.

What Happens to Live Photos?

A Live Photo is a still image plus a short video clip stored in the same HEIC file. The converter exports the still image as a JPG and discards the video motion.

That is the right behaviour for most uses. A form, a website, or a printout needs the still photo, not a three-second video.

If you want to keep the motion, do not convert the Live Photo. Share it from the Photos app instead, where the video part survives.

The still frame chosen is the key photo, which is the frame you see when you open the Live Photo. It is the best single frame of the clip.

Orientation, Colour, and Resolution

Orientation is preserved during conversion. A vertical photo stays vertical, and a horizontal photo stays horizontal, because the converter reads the orientation data in the HEIC file.

Resolution is preserved too. A 4032 by 3024 HEIC becomes a 4032 by 3024 JPG, with no pixels lost in the conversion.

Wide-gamut Display P3 colours are converted to standard colours for the JPG. The shift is subtle, and most photos look the same, but very saturated colours may look slightly less vivid.

GPS location tags are stripped during conversion, since drawing onto a canvas removes EXIF metadata. That protects your privacy when sharing photos online.

Converting Whole Albums at Once

You can drop an entire vacation album into the converter and download one ZIP. Every HEIC converts to JPG with the same settings, and the batch summary shows the total size.

Each file converts independently, so one bad file cannot stop the album. A corrupted HEIC shows an error badge, while the rest finish normally.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted, which covers standard 12-megapixel, 24-megapixel, and 48-megapixel iPhone captures without any trouble.

After converting, keep the HEIC originals. They are the best-quality copies you own, and you can always convert them again if you need a different format later.

HEIC vs HEIF: What Is the Difference?

HEIF is the container format, and HEIC is the version of it that uses HEVC video compression for the image data. In practice, iPhone photos are HEIC files with a .heic extension.

Some files use the .heif extension instead. The converter accepts both, and treats them the same way, since the underlying structure is identical for photos.

You do not need to rename files or worry about the extension. Drop the file in, and the decoder handles whichever variant it is.

For everyday use, the two terms mean the same thing: an Apple photo format that needs converting before most of the world can open it.

Stopping Future Photos from Saving as HEIC

Open Settings, then Camera, then Formats on your iPhone. Choose Most Compatible, and future photos will save as JPG instead of HEIC.

The trade-off is storage space. JPG photos take roughly twice the room of HEIC photos, so your camera roll will fill up faster.

Most people are better off leaving HEIC on and converting when needed. The space savings are real, and conversion takes only seconds.

If you share photos with Windows users often, switching to JPG saves everyone the conversion step. Choose based on who receives your photos most.

Common Problems and Fixes

If a file fails with an invalid HEIC error, the file may be corrupted or may not be a real HEIC at all. Try opening it on your iPhone first to confirm it is a valid photo.

If decoding is slow, the file is probably a 48-megapixel ProRAW capture. Those files carry far more data than a standard photo, so give the decoder a few extra seconds.

If the converted JPG looks dull, check the colour. Display P3 colours shift slightly during conversion, and a small saturation adjustment in any photo app restores the punch.

If you need the JPG under 100KB for a portal, convert first and then compress. The 100KB compressor takes the converted JPG and shrinks it to fit.

Sharing iPhone Photos with Family

Family members use a mix of devices, and HEIC photos fail on many of them. A grandparent on an old Windows laptop cannot open the photo you just sent, and the moment is lost.

Convert the photos to JPG before sharing them. A JPG opens on every device, from a ten-year-old laptop to a brand-new phone, and nobody needs to install anything.

For a family album, convert the whole set at once and share the ZIP or upload the JPGs to a shared album. Everyone sees the same photos, regardless of their device.

Keep the quality at 90 percent for family photos. The faces stay sharp, the colours stay rich, and the files are still small enough to send by email or chat.

After sharing, keep the HEIC originals on your phone. They are the best-quality copies, and you can convert them again if a different family member needs a different format.

HEIC Photos in Work Documents

Work documents often need a photo, and a HEIC file pasted into Word or PowerPoint may fail. The document looks fine on your Mac but breaks on a colleague's Windows machine.

Convert the photo to JPG before adding it to the document. The JPG embeds cleanly in Word, PowerPoint, and Google Docs, and the document opens correctly everywhere.

For a report with many photos, convert the whole set first, then build the document. That order prevents the frustrating discovery that half the images are broken after hours of work.

Check the file size after converting. A report with twenty 4MB JPGs is 80MB, which may exceed email limits, so compress the images if the document must be sent by email.

For a printed report, keep the quality at 90 percent or higher. Print reveals flaws that screens hide, and a high-quality JPG is the safest choice for paper.

Printing iPhone Photos

Print shops expect JPG or PNG, and a HEIC file may be rejected outright. Convert to JPG before ordering prints, and check the shop's requirements first.

Keep the quality at 90 percent or higher for prints. A photo printed at 6 by 4 inches needs every pixel of detail, and a low-quality JPG shows its flaws on paper.

Check the dimensions before printing. A 4032 by 3024 iPhone photo prints beautifully at 13 by 10 inches, but a cropped version may need a smaller print size.

For large prints, use the full-resolution HEIC original as the conversion source. A JPG converted from a thumbnail will never print sharply, no matter what quality you choose.

Order a test print for an important photo. A single small print confirms the colours and sharpness before you commit to a large order or a canvas.

Moving Photos to a New Phone

Moving to a new phone is the moment when HEIC compatibility matters most. An Android phone may not display the HEIC photos transferred from an iPhone without conversion.

Convert the photos to JPG before transferring them. The JPGs open on any Android phone, any Windows computer, and any cloud service, without special software.

For a full camera roll, convert in batches of fifty to a hundred. The batches run smoothly, and a failure in one batch does not affect the others.

Keep the HEIC originals backed up in iCloud or on a computer before the move. If the transfer goes wrong, the originals are safe, and you can convert them again.

After the move, check a few photos on the new phone. Open them, confirm they display correctly, and check that the dates and locations survived the transfer.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert HEIC to JPG on a Windows computer?

Yes. Open the converter in any modern Windows browser, such as Chrome or Edge, and drop in the HEIC files. The decoding runs inside the browser tab, so Windows itself never needs to understand HEIC. The converted JPGs download normally and open in any Windows program.

Do I lose photo quality converting HEIC to JPG?

A little, because JPG is a lossier format than HEIC, but at 90 percent quality the loss is invisible in normal viewing. The photo keeps its full resolution and looks the same on screen. Only pixel-level inspection reveals any difference.

Can I convert HEIC photos directly on my iPhone?

Yes. Open the converter in Safari or Chrome on your iPhone, tap the upload area, and pick photos from your camera roll. The conversion runs on the phone, and the JPGs download to your Files app. No extra app is needed.

What is the best quality setting for HEIC to JPG conversion?

90 percent is the default and the best choice for most photos. It preserves fine detail while keeping the file reasonable. Drop to 80 percent only when a file size limit forces it, and keep the HEIC original so you can convert again later.

Will converting HEIC to JPG remove the location data from my photos?

Yes. Drawing the photo onto a canvas strips EXIF metadata, including GPS coordinates, camera model, and timestamps. The converted JPG carries no record of where or when it was taken, which is safer for sharing online.

Can I convert a HEIC file to PNG instead of JPG?

Yes, the tool also offers PNG and WebP output. PNG is lossless, so it preserves the decoded pixels exactly, but the file is much larger than a JPG. Use PNG only when you need transparency or plan to edit the image many times.
