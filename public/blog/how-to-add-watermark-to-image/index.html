How to Add a Watermark to an Image

Add a watermark to any image in minutes: text or logo, placement, opacity, size, and tiled patterns, with a clean export at the end.

Adding a watermark takes only a few minutes, and it protects your work wherever the image travels. You do not need design software or an account, and the whole job runs in your browser.

This how-to walks through the process step by step. It covers text and logo watermarks, placement, opacity, size, and the tiled pattern that protects client proofs.

By the end you will know how to mark a single photo, a batch of proofs, and the mistakes that make a watermark useless. Keep your unwatermarked original safe until you have checked the result.

Before You Start: What You Need

You need the image file and, if you want a logo watermark, the logo file too. A transparent PNG logo works best, because its empty areas let the photo show through.

Decide what the watermark should say before you open the tool. A name, a website, or a short phrase is enough, and shorter text is easier to read at small sizes.

Think about your goal as well. A proof needs a strong, hard-to-remove mark, while a finished image for social media needs only a light brand in a corner.

Open the Image Watermark tool in your browser. Everything runs locally, so your photos and your logo are never uploaded to a server.

Add a Watermark in Six Steps

The process is the same for a text watermark and a logo watermark. Upload the photo, set the mark, place it, check the preview, and download the result.

Take your time with placement and opacity. Those two settings decide whether the watermark protects the image or merely decorates it.

  1. Upload your photo by dragging it into the dropzone, choosing it from your device, or pasting it with Ctrl+V.
  2. Type your watermark text, or upload your transparent PNG logo using the logo option.
  3. Pick a position using the anchor presets, or drag the watermark to an exact custom spot.
  4. Set the opacity, size, and rotation angle, and switch on the tiled repeat pattern for client proofs.
  5. Preview the result on light and dark parts of the image to confirm the mark stays readable.
  6. Choose the export format and quality, then click Download to save the watermarked image.

Open the downloaded file once before you share it. Confirm the watermark reads clearly and the photo underneath still looks good.

Choosing Between a Text and a Logo Watermark

A text watermark is the fastest option. Type your name or your website, and the tool renders it over the photo in seconds, with no extra files needed.

A logo watermark looks more professional, especially for photographers and studios. Upload a transparent PNG of your brand mark, and it appears over the photo exactly as designed.

Text is better when the goal is a clear copyright notice. A name and a year leave no doubt about ownership, while a logo alone may not be recognised by a stranger.

You can use both at once. A small logo in a corner with your website written beneath it gives branding and attribution in a single mark.

Typing Your Watermark Text

Keep the text short. A name, a studio name, or a website address is enough, and short text stays readable even at a small size.

Avoid long sentences in a watermark. A full copyright notice is hard to read over a busy photo, and it covers more of the image than it needs to.

Use your real name or your business name, not a nickname that nobody recognises. The watermark is meant to identify you, so it should say who you are.

If you leave the text input blank, the tool has nothing to render as a text mark. Type the text first, then adjust the size and the position.

Uploading Your Logo as a Watermark

Prepare your logo as a transparent PNG before you start. The transparency lets the photo show through the empty areas, so only the logo itself appears over the image.

A JPG logo has a solid white background that shows as a rectangle over your photo. It looks unprofessional, and it is easy to spot as a mistake.

Upload the logo through the logo option in the tool. You can replace it later without reloading the page, which is useful when you want to test a different mark.

Check the logo at the size you will use it. A detailed logo that looks fine at full size may turn into a blur at a small watermark size, so simplify it if needed.

Choosing Where the Watermark Goes

Anchor presets place the mark in a standard spot: a corner, an edge, or the centre. Corners are the least distracting, while the centre is the hardest to remove.

Drag the watermark to an exact custom spot when the presets do not fit. You can place the mark over a plain area of the photo, where it is readable without covering the subject.

For branding, a bottom corner is usually best. It marks the image clearly without covering the subject, and viewers expect to find a logo there.

For protection, the centre or a diagonal position is better. A mark in the middle cannot be cropped away without destroying the photo itself.

Setting the Opacity

Opacity controls how strong the watermark looks. At 100 percent it is solid and blocks the image beneath, while at 10 percent it is a faint ghost that is easy to miss.

For client proofs, use 30 to 50 percent. The mark is clearly readable, which deters misuse, but the client can still judge the photo underneath it.

For branding on a finished image, 15 to 25 percent is usually enough. The logo is visible without distracting from the photo, and it does not fight the subject for attention.

Test the opacity on both light and dark parts of the image. A mark that is clear on a dark background may vanish on a bright sky, and the reverse is also true.

Setting the Watermark Size

Watermark text is sized as a percentage of the image, not as a fixed pixel count. The same setting looks consistent on a small thumbnail and a huge camera photo.

A size that looks right on a large photo may be invisible on a small one if it is set in pixels. Percentage sizing avoids that problem entirely.

Check the size on the actual dimensions you will share. A mark that looks subtle at full size may dominate a small preview, and a mark sized for a thumbnail may vanish on a large print.

When you watermark a batch of different-sized photos, percentage sizing keeps the result consistent across all of them without any extra work from you.

Setting the Rotation Angle

A slight angle, usually around 30 to 45 degrees, makes a watermark harder to remove and less like a caption. A diagonal mark crosses more of the image than a horizontal one.

For proofs, a diagonal mark is the stronger choice. It passes over the centre and the edges, so no single crop can remove it cleanly.

For branding, keep the angle at zero. A straight logo in a corner looks professional, while a tilted logo looks like a mistake that slipped through.

Adjust the angle in small steps and watch the preview. A few degrees can make the difference between a mark that looks deliberate and one that looks accidental.

Using the Tiled Repeat Pattern

A single watermark in a corner is easy to remove, because anyone can crop off a corner or paint over one small mark. A tiled pattern solves that problem.

Tiled mode places the watermark again and again across the whole image in a grid, so no crop can remove all of it. It is the standard for client proofs.

When tiled mode is on, the anchor presets hide, because the pattern covers the whole frame by design. You control the spacing and the angle instead.

Use a lower opacity for tiled marks than for single marks. A repeated pattern at high opacity overwhelms the photo, while a light repeat protects it without hiding it.

Previewing Before You Download

Always preview before downloading. The preview shows exactly how the watermark will look on the exported file, including its size, opacity, and position.

Check the mark on the busiest part of the photo. A watermark that reads clearly on a plain wall may vanish in a forest, a crowd, or a patterned fabric.

Check it on the brightest and the darkest areas too. A white mark disappears on a bright sky, and a dark mark disappears in a shadow, so test both extremes.

If the mark is unreadable somewhere important, change its colour, add an outline, or move it to a plainer area. Fixing it now is faster than re-doing the whole job.

Choosing the Export Format

Choose JPG for photos you plan to share or send to clients. It keeps file sizes small and works everywhere, including old forms and messaging apps.

Choose PNG when the base image is a transparent graphic or a logo. PNG preserves transparency, so the see-through areas stay see-through in the export.

Choose WebP when you want small files with good quality. It works well for web images, though a few older systems still reject the format.

Remember that the quality slider hides for PNG, because PNG is lossless and has no quality setting to adjust. That is normal behaviour, not a problem with the tool.

Watermarking on a Phone or Tablet

The tool works on touchscreens, so you can watermark a photo on the phone that took it. Upload from your gallery, type the text, and drag the mark into place with your finger.

Positioning with a finger is less precise than with a mouse, so use the anchor presets for exact corners. They place the mark perfectly without any dragging.

Check the preview at full size before downloading. A mark that looks fine on a small phone screen may be too large or too faint when viewed on a computer.

Downloaded files save to your usual downloads folder. From there you can share them directly to a client, an email, or a social post.

Troubleshooting Common Watermark Problems

If the watermark is invisible, the opacity is probably too low or the colour matches the background. Raise the opacity or switch the colour and check the preview again.

If the watermark covers the subject, move it to a plainer area or reduce its size. A mark that hides the face or the product defeats the purpose of the photo.

If the anchor presets are missing, check whether tiled mode is on. The presets hide in tiled mode because the pattern covers the whole frame, and they return when you switch it off.

If the logo shows a white box, the logo file is a JPG, not a transparent PNG. Replace it with a transparent PNG and the white rectangle will disappear.

Quick Tips for Better Watermarks

Watermark a copy, never the original. Once a mark is flattened into an export, it cannot be removed from that file, so the original is your only clean version.

Keep the text short and the logo simple. A mark that is easy to read at a glance protects better than a detailed one that turns into a blur.

Test the watermark on a busy photo before you apply it to a whole batch. A mark that works on a plain background may fail on a complicated scene.

Save your settings by noting them down: opacity, size, angle, and position. A short note lets you reproduce the same mark on every image you publish.

Watermarking a Batch of Photos

A wedding or an event can produce hundreds of photos that all need the same mark. Watermark them one at a time, keeping the settings identical across the set.

Write your settings down before you start: the text or logo, the opacity, the size, the angle, and the position. A short note keeps every photo in the batch consistent.

Work through the batch in order and preview every tenth photo. A quick check catches a setting that drifted before it affects dozens of files.

Save the watermarked batch in a folder separate from the originals. A clear folder name, such as proofs-watermarked, prevents you from mixing marked and clean files.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add a watermark to a photo on my phone?

Open the Image Watermark tool in your phone browser, upload the photo from your gallery, and type your text or upload a transparent PNG logo. Use the anchor presets for exact placement, set the opacity, preview the result, and download the file.

What is the best watermark position for a photo?

A bottom corner is best for branding, since it marks the image without covering the subject. The centre or a diagonal position is best for protection, since it cannot be cropped away. Choose based on whether you want branding or protection.

Can I watermark multiple photos at once?

The tool handles one photo at a time, so watermark each image separately and keep the settings consistent. Note down your opacity, size, angle, and position so every photo in the set carries the same mark.

Why is my watermark invisible on some parts of the photo?

The mark colour probably matches the background in those areas. White text vanishes on a bright sky and dark text vanishes in shadows. Change the colour, add an outline, or move the mark to a plainer area of the image.

How do I make a watermark that cannot be cropped out?

Use the tiled repeat pattern, which places the watermark across the whole image in a grid. No single crop can remove all of it. Combine the tile with a diagonal angle and a moderate opacity for the strongest protection.

Does adding a watermark change the image file size?

Only slightly. The watermark adds a small amount of data, but the file size depends mostly on the export format and quality setting. A JPG export at a high quality setting stays close to the original size.
