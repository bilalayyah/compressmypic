How to Blur or Pixelate an Image

Blur or pixelate any image in minutes: paint over faces and text with a brush, or blur the whole image, then export a clean censored copy.

Hiding a face or an address in a photo takes only a few minutes. You paint over the sensitive part with a brush, check the result, and download the censored copy.

This how-to walks through the whole process. It covers brush and whole-image modes, blur and pixelation, brush size, and the checks that confirm nothing is still readable.

By the end you will be able to censor a face, a number plate, a screenshot, or a document cleanly on any device. Keep the uncensored original separate until you have checked the download.

Before You Start: List What Needs Hiding

Open the original photo and list everything sensitive in it: faces, addresses, number plates, screens, documents, and names on badges. Check the background as well as the foreground.

Look for reflections too. A face can appear in a mirror, a window, or a pair of sunglasses, and every copy of it needs censoring, not just the main one.

Decide whether to blur or pixelate before you start. Pixelation is safer for text and numbers, while a strong blur works well for faces and backgrounds.

Open the Blur and Pixelate Image tool in your browser. Everything runs locally, so your photo is never uploaded to a server.

Blur or Pixelate an Image in Six Steps

The process is the same for a face, a number plate, or a screenshot. Upload, choose the mode, paint or apply, check, and download.

Take your time with the painting step. A careful brush stroke now saves you from discovering a readable detail after you have already shared the image.

  1. Upload your image by dragging it into the dropzone, choosing it from your device, or pasting a screenshot with Ctrl+V.
  2. Choose Brush Area mode to paint over specific details, or Whole Image mode to blur the entire photo.
  3. Pick Smooth Blur or Pixelate Mosaic, then set the brush size and the strength or block size.
  4. Paint over each sensitive area with the brush, covering faces fully and text generously.
  5. Zoom in and preview the result to confirm nothing is still readable or recognisable.
  6. Choose the export format and quality, then click Download to save the censored image.

Open the downloaded file once before you share it. Check it at full size and confirm every sensitive detail is hidden.

Choosing Between Blur and Pixelation

Choose Smooth Blur for faces and backgrounds. A strong blur removes the features while keeping the photo looking natural, and it suits portraits well.

Choose Pixelate Mosaic for text, numbers, and addresses. The mosaic blocks destroy letter shapes completely, while a soft blur can leave their outlines readable.

You can mix both on the same image. Paint a face with blur and a number plate with pixelation, using the mode switch between strokes.

When in doubt, use pixelation. A mosaic that is slightly too strong looks intentional, while a blur that is slightly too weak leaves the detail readable.

Using Brush Area Mode

Brush Area mode is the precise option. You paint the effect exactly where you need it, and the rest of the image stays sharp and untouched.

Start with a large brush for the main area, such as a whole face. Cover the head fully, including the hairline, the ears, and the chin, not just the eyes.

Switch to a smaller brush for the edges. A small brush lets you trace around a single word or clean up the border of a censored area without touching the rest.

The undo button removes your last stroke, and the reset button clears all brush strokes. Use them freely; it is faster to redo a stroke than to share a badly censored photo.

Using Whole Image Mode

Whole Image mode applies the effect to the entire photo at once. It is the right choice when the whole image is sensitive, or when you want a blurred background for a design.

Switch the target area to Whole Image and the tool applies your current blur or pixelation strength to the full frame. The colours and the mood remain, but no detail survives.

A fully blurred photo makes a good background for text or a presentation slide. The image adds colour without distracting from the words on top of it.

Do not use whole-image mode for a photo you want people to see clearly. A fully blurred photo hides the subject as well as the sensitive details, so it is rarely the right choice for sharing.

Setting the Brush Size

Brush size should match the detail you are hiding. A face needs a large brush, a number plate needs a medium one, and a single word needs a small one.

A brush that is too large censors more than you intended. It can cover a neighbouring face or a useful part of the image that did not need hiding.

A brush that is too small takes many strokes to cover a large area, and the gaps between strokes can leave slivers of the sensitive detail visible.

Adjust the size as you work. Start large for the main area, then switch small to clean up the edges and cover any missed spots.

Setting Blur Strength and Block Size

Blur strength and mosaic block size both control how much information survives. Higher values hide more, and lower values hide less.

For faces, use a strong blur or large blocks. A light blur that leaves the eyes and the smile visible does not protect anyone, and small blocks still show the face shape.

For small screenshots, a moderate block size is enough. The text is small, so even a modest mosaic destroys the letter shapes completely.

Test the result by zooming in. If you can still read the text or recognise the face at full size, increase the strength and check again.

Fixing Mistakes While Painting

If a stroke goes wrong, use the undo button to remove it. Undo works stroke by stroke, so you can step back one move at a time without losing the good work.

If the whole censoring looks wrong, use the reset button to clear all brush strokes. Starting again is faster than trying to repair a messy set of strokes.

Watch for gaps between strokes. A thin sliver of visible text between two brush strokes can give away the hidden word, so overlap your strokes slightly.

Check the edges of each censored area. A face with a visible ear or a number plate with a visible digit is not properly hidden, even if the middle is covered.

Previewing Before You Download

Always preview before downloading. Zoom into each censored area at full size and confirm that no feature, letter, or number is still readable.

Check the background as well as the foreground. A reflection in a window or a mirror can show the very face or address you just censored in the main image.

Look at the image on a larger screen if you can. A detail that looks hidden on a phone may be readable on a computer monitor, where the image is shown bigger.

If anything is still readable, increase the strength or add another stroke. Fixing it now is far easier than recalling an image you already shared.

Choosing the Export Format

Choose JPG for photos you plan to share. It keeps file sizes small and works everywhere, and the censored areas compress cleanly.

Choose PNG for screenshots and graphics with text. PNG is lossless, so the sharp edges of the uncensored parts stay crisp, and it preserves transparency.

Choose WebP when you want small files with good quality. It works well for web images, though a few older systems still reject the format.

Remember that the quality slider hides for PNG, because PNG is lossless and has no quality setting to adjust. That is normal behaviour, not a problem with the tool.

Blurring on a Phone or Tablet

The tool works with touch, so you can censor a photo on the phone that took it. Upload from your gallery and paint over the sensitive areas with your finger.

Use a larger brush on a small screen. Finger painting is less precise than a mouse, so a bigger brush covers the area reliably without needing many strokes.

Zoom in before you paint small details. A number plate or a single word needs a close look, and zooming makes the strokes accurate even on a phone.

Downloaded files save to your usual downloads folder. From there you can share the censored image directly to a chat app, an email, or a social post.

Blurring a Screenshot

Paste a screenshot directly with Ctrl+V to skip the save step. The image appears in the tool immediately, ready to censor.

Paint over names, email addresses, passwords, and internal URLs. A screenshot of a dashboard often reveals more than the part you meant to share.

Use pixelation for text in screenshots. The mosaic destroys the letter shapes completely, while a soft blur can leave the words partly readable at full size.

Export screenshots as PNG. The lossless format keeps the uncensored text sharp, which matters when the screenshot is meant to show a setting or an error message.

Blurring a Document Photo

Document photos often contain names, addresses, and ID numbers in several places. List them all before you start painting, so none of them is missed.

Cover the whole line of text, not just the sensitive word. The length of the line and the words around it can give away the hidden part.

Check the edges of the document photo. A second page, a reflection, or a shadow can carry the same information in a place you did not expect.

For documents you must share, consider cropping away the sensitive parts entirely. A crop removes the data permanently, while a blur only hides it in that one file.

Troubleshooting Common Censoring Problems

If a face is still recognisable, the blur is too light or the blocks are too small. Increase the strength and paint over the face again until no feature is visible.

If text is still readable, switch from blur to pixelation. A mosaic with large blocks destroys letter shapes far more reliably than a soft blur.

If the undo button is missing, check whether you are in Whole Image mode. The undo and reset buttons appear only in Brush Area mode, where strokes exist to undo.

If the preview looks different from the download, check the export format and quality. A low JPG quality setting can soften the whole image, including the parts you wanted sharp.

Quick Tips for Safer Censoring

Censor generously. Covering a little extra is harmless, while leaving a sliver of a face or a digit visible defeats the whole purpose.

Zoom in after every stroke. A quick zoom check catches gaps and missed spots before they become a shared mistake.

Share only the censored file. Keep the original in a separate folder, and never attach it to the same message or post as the censored version.

Check the metadata after export. The tool strips GPS tags, but confirm the downloaded file carries no location record before you share it publicly.

Blurring a Photo for a Presentation

A blurred photo makes a clean slide background. The soft colours add visual interest, while the text on top stays sharp and easy to read from the back of the room.

Use Whole Image mode with a moderate blur, then place your title and bullet points over it. The blur should be strong enough that the text is readable, but light enough that the photo still shows.

Keep the slide text large and high contrast. White text on a dark blurred photo works well, while dark text needs a light blurred background to stay readable.

Export the background as JPG at a high quality setting. A clean background keeps the whole slide looking professional, while a low quality export softens the text as well.

Frequently asked questions

How do I blur just one face in a group photo?

Use Brush Area mode and paint over only that face with a large brush and a strong blur or mosaic. The rest of the photo stays sharp. Zoom in afterwards to confirm the whole head is covered, including the hairline and ears.

Can I blur and pixelate different parts of the same image?

Yes. Paint the face with Smooth Blur, then switch to Pixelate Mosaic and paint over the text or number plate. You can mix both effects on one image by changing the mode between strokes.

What brush size should I use for a number plate?

A medium brush that covers the plate in two or three strokes works well. Use pixelation with a moderate to large block size, since text needs the mosaic to destroy the letter shapes. Zoom in to confirm no digit is still readable.

How do I blur a screenshot on my phone?

Open the tool in your phone browser, upload the screenshot from your gallery, and choose Brush Area mode. Paint over names and passwords with your finger, using a large brush and pixelation for text, then download the censored copy.

Why are the undo and reset buttons missing?

They appear only in Brush Area mode, where strokes exist to undo. In Whole Image mode there are no brush strokes, so the buttons hide. Switch back to Brush Area mode to paint and to use undo and reset.

Is it safe to share a blurred photo of my child online?

A strongly blurred or pixelated face is much safer than a visible one, but no method is perfect. Use a large mosaic or a heavy blur, cover the whole head, and check the result at full size. Avoid sharing the original file anywhere.
