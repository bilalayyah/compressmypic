How to Convert BMP to JPG

Convert BMP bitmaps to JPG in a few steps. Upload, set quality, preview the savings, and download — one file or a whole folder of scans.

A BMP file from old Windows software can weigh twenty times more than the same image as a JPG. Converting it takes less than a minute, and the result is far easier to send, upload, and store.

There is no transparency to worry about, since BMP files are opaque, and the only decision is the quality setting.

This guide walks through each step, so your first conversion comes out clean.

Before You Start: What You Need

You need the BMP files and a modern browser. Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari all work, on desktop and on mobile.

Find the files on your computer. They usually come from old Windows software, scanners, or digital cameras that save in BMP format.

Know what the JPGs are for. A form may demand a maximum file size, while an archive wants the highest quality. The purpose decides the settings.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers large screenshots and high-resolution scans without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the BMP to JPG Tool

Open the BMP to JPG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS.

You will see an upload area, a quality slider, and a convert button. Those three controls are the whole job.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for work scans and personal files.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick BMP files from your device.

Step 2: Upload Your BMP Files

Drag your BMP files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select many files at once for a batch.

Each file appears as a card with a thumbnail, its dimensions, and its size. A quick scan confirms the right files loaded and nothing extra slipped in.

Old 1-bit and 8-bit BMP files are supported, as are standard 24-bit files. The converter reads the palette and the pixel data for every variant.

You can also paste a copied image with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when the BMP is in your clipboard from another app.

Step 3: Set the Quality Slider

For scanned documents, set the quality to 85 to 90 percent. Small print stays readable, and the file still shrinks by 90 percent or more.

For screenshots, 85 percent is the sweet spot. The interface stays crisp, and the file is a fraction of the BMP size.

For photographs saved as BMP, 80 to 85 percent is the right range. The photo looks clean, and the file shrinks dramatically.

Do not go below 75 percent for anything with text. Small letters blur quickly at low quality, and a blurry scan is worse than a large one.

Step 4: Convert the Files

Click the Convert button. The tool decodes each BMP, draws it onto a canvas, and encodes it as a JPG.

Conversion is fast, usually a second or two per file. Larger files take a little longer, since there are more pixels to encode.

Each card updates with the new JPG size next to the old BMP size. A good conversion shows a 90 percent or greater saving on most files.

If a file fails, read the error message. An unusual BMP variant is the usual cause, and the rest of the batch keeps converting.

Step 5: Preview Before Downloading

Click Compare on any card to see the BMP and the JPG side by side. Drag the split handle across the image to inspect text and fine detail.

Check the smallest text first, since it suffers most from JPG compression. A scanned document at 85 percent quality should keep its print perfectly readable.

Look at the colours too. Most BMP files keep their colours accurately, though unusual palettes may shift slightly during conversion.

If the result looks soft, raise the quality by five points and convert again. The original BMP stays in the queue, so you never re-upload.

Step 6: Download the JPG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one JPG. The filename keeps your original name with a .jpg extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted JPG inside. That is far quicker than downloading them one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a JPG in your photo viewer, confirm it looks right, and check that it opens where it needs to.

Keep the original BMP files as well. A JPG can never become a perfect BMP again, so the originals are your safety net.

  2. Set the quality slider to 85 percent.
  3. Convert and check the text with Compare.
  4. Download single JPGs or the whole batch as a ZIP.

Converting a Folder of BMP Scans

Select all the BMP files in the folder at once. They load into the queue, and one click converts them all to JPG.

Use the same quality setting for the whole folder. Consistency matters when the scans belong to the same archive or document set.

Watch the batch summary after converting. A folder of BMP scans often shrinks by 95 percent overall, which is a dramatic number.

Unzip the results into a fresh folder, not on top of the originals. A separate folder keeps the converted files organised and prevents accidental overwrites.

Fixing Common Problems

If a BMP file fails to convert, open it in Windows Paint first and re-save it as a standard BMP. Unusual variants from old software sometimes use unsupported features.

If the text looks blurry, the quality was too low. Raise the slider to 90 percent and convert again from the original BMP.

If the JPG is barely smaller than the BMP, the image is probably very simple or very noisy. A simple image has little to compress, while noise resists compression.

If the colours look wrong, the BMP may use an unusual palette. Check the result carefully, and convert from a fresh export if the source file is suspect.

After Converting: What to Do Next

If the JPGs are for a form, check the size limit before uploading. A converted JPG at 85 percent quality may still exceed a strict cap, and you may need to compress it further.

Use the 100KB compressor or the target file size compressor when a portal demands an exact size. Convert first, then compress, in that order.

If the JPGs are for an archive, store them with clear names. A name such as invoice-2024-03.jpg is useful years later, while a name such as scan001.jpg is not.

If the JPGs are for a website, consider converting them to WebP as well. The JPG to WebP converter shrinks them further for fast pages.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick BMP files from your device. The conversion runs on the phone, and the JPGs download to your device.

Keep phone batches small, since large BMP files take time to encode on a mobile processor. Ten files at a time is comfortable.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large folder of scans, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make many files far easier to manage.

Converting Scans for an Archive

An archive of scanned documents is a common BMP collection. Hundreds of pages saved as BMP fill a drive, while the same pages as JPG fit on a fraction of the space.

Convert the scans in batches of fifty, using 85 to 90 percent quality. The text stays readable, the files shrink by 95 percent, and the archive becomes manageable.

Sort the scans by year or by subject before converting. A sorted archive is easy to navigate, while a single folder of a thousand files is not.

Name the converted files by document and date, such as contract-2024-03.jpg. A clear name helps anyone find the right file without opening ten candidates.

Keep the BMP originals in a separate archive folder after converting. They are the masters, and you can always convert them again if you need a different format later.

Converting BMP for Email

Email attachments have size limits, usually 20MB to 25MB, and a single BMP scan can exceed them. Converting to JPG first keeps the email under the limit.

For a single scan, convert it to JPG at 85 percent quality and attach the result. The file is a fraction of the BMP size, and the recipient sees the same document.

For several scans, convert the batch and attach the JPGs together. A set of five BMP scans might be 50MB, while the JPG versions total 2MB.

For a very large set, zip the JPGs into a single archive after converting. One attachment is easier to manage than ten, and the recipient downloads everything at once.

Check the total size before sending. A quick glance at the attachment sizes prevents the frustrating bounce message that arrives after you thought the email was sent.

Converting BMP for a Form

Upload forms list JPG and PNG as accepted formats, and a BMP file bounces straight back. Convert to JPG before uploading, and check the form rules first.

Convert the image to JPG at 85 percent quality. The document looks clean, the file is a reasonable size, and the form accepts it without any trouble.

Check the file size limit after converting. A form that caps uploads at 2MB may reject a 4MB JPG, and you may need to compress it with the 100KB or 200KB tool.

Check the dimensions as well as the format. A form that demands a specific pixel size will reject an image that is the right format but the wrong shape.

Upload the converted image early, before the deadline. A last-minute conversion leaves no time to fix a problem, while an early upload leaves room to try again.

Converting BMP for a Website

Websites rarely accept BMP uploads, and a BMP image on a web page loads slowly. Convert to JPG before uploading, and the page works properly.

Convert the BMP to JPG at 80 to 85 percent quality for the web. The image looks clean, the file is small, and the page loads fast for every visitor.

For a website image with text, consider WebP instead. The text stays sharper than JPG at the same size, which matters for screenshots and diagrams.

Check the image after converting, using the Compare viewer. Zoom into the smallest text and confirm it reads cleanly before publishing the page.

Keep the BMP original in the media library as a fallback. If the website needs a different format later, the original is ready without a fresh export.

Converting a Folder of Old Files

An old folder of BMP files is a common discovery on a shared drive. Hundreds of forgotten scans and screenshots, each several megabytes, filling space for no reason.

Sort the folder first, removing duplicates and files nobody needs. A folder of five hundred files often has a hundred worth keeping, and converting only those saves real time.

Convert the keepers in batches of fifty, using 85 percent quality. Each batch runs smoothly, and a failure in one batch does not affect the others.

Store the converted JPGs in a new folder with the same structure as the original. A mirrored structure keeps the archive navigable for everyone.

Delete the BMP files only after verifying the JPGs. Check a sample of the converted files first, confirm they look right, and then free the disk space.

Converting BMP for a School Project

School projects often need images from old software, and those images are usually BMP. A school computer with old software may display them, but the project file becomes enormous.

Convert the BMP images to JPG before adding them to the project. The JPGs embed cleanly in PowerPoint, Word, and Google Slides, and the project file stays manageable.

For a project with many images, convert the whole set at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the JPGs are ready to drop into the slides.

Check the images after converting. A quick look at each one confirms it displays correctly, so the project has no broken images on presentation day.

Cite the image sources in the project. A converted image still belongs to its creator, and a proper credit shows good academic practice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I batch-convert a folder full of BMP scans?

Select all the BMP files in the folder at once in the file picker, or drag the folder onto the upload area. Set the quality to 85 percent, convert, and download the ZIP. Unzip into a fresh folder so the converted JPGs never overwrite the original BMPs.

Can I convert BMP files without an internet connection once the page is open?

Yes. Once the page has loaded, the conversion runs locally in your browser and does not need the internet. You need a connection only to open the page the first time. After that, you can convert BMP files offline until you close the tab.

Will the JPG open in any program?

Yes. JPG is the most compatible image format in existence, and it opens in Windows Photos, macOS Preview, WhatsApp, older printers, and every browser and editor. The converted JPG is a standard file with no special requirements.

Can I adjust the quality slider after the first conversion?

Yes. The original BMP files stay in the queue after the first conversion, so you can move the quality slider and convert again without re-uploading. Download each version before switching settings, since the download button saves the current conversion.

Does BMP to JPG work on mobile browsers?

Yes. Open the tool in your phone browser, pick BMP files from your device, and download the JPGs. The conversion runs on the phone, and the files download to your device. Keep phone batches small, since large BMP files take time to encode on mobile.

What should I do if a BMP file fails to convert?

Open it in Windows Paint first and re-save it as a standard BMP. Unusual variants from old software sometimes use unsupported compression or colour depths. If the file still fails, check that it is a real BMP and not a different format with a .bmp extension.
