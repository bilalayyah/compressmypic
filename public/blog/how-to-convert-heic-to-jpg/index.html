How to Convert HEIC to JPG

Convert iPhone HEIC photos to JPG in a few taps. Upload, check the preview, and download — on any device, without installing anything.

Your iPhone takes beautiful photos and saves them in a format half the world cannot open. Converting those HEIC files to JPG takes less than a minute, and the result works everywhere.

No app to install, no account to create, and no upload to a server. The conversion runs in your browser, on your own device.

This guide walks through each step, from picking the photos to downloading the finished JPGs.

Before You Start: What You Need

You need the HEIC files and a modern browser. Chrome, Safari, Edge, and Firefox all work, on a computer, a phone, or a tablet.

Find the photos in your camera roll or your files. On iPhone, they are the photos your camera saved; on a computer, they are the .heic files someone sent you.

Know what the JPGs are for. A form may demand a maximum file size, while a print shop may want the highest quality. The purpose decides the settings.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers every standard iPhone photo, including 48-megapixel ProRAW captures, without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the HEIC to JPG Tool

Open the HEIC to JPG tool in your browser. The page is simple: an upload area, a quality slider, and a download button.

The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

If you are on an iPhone, open the page in Safari. It is already there, and the conversion runs directly on the phone.

The tool loads a small decoder the first time you use it. That takes a second, and after that the conversion is fast.

Step 2: Upload Your HEIC Photos

Drag your HEIC files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select several photos at once.

On iPhone, tap the upload area and choose photos from your library. On Android, pick them from your gallery or file manager.

Each photo appears as a card with a thumbnail, so you can confirm the right images loaded. A wrong file is easy to spot and remove.

You can also paste a copied image with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when someone sends you a HEIC file in a chat and you want it converted immediately.

Step 3: Check the Quality Setting

The quality slider defaults to 90 percent, which is the right setting for most photos. It preserves fine detail while keeping the file reasonable.

Leave it at 90 unless you have a reason to change it. A form with a strict size limit is the usual reason, and even then, change it gradually.

Lower the slider to 80 percent if the JPGs come out too large. That step often halves the file size with little visible change.

Raise it to 95 percent only for photos you plan to print. Print shows flaws that screens hide, and the extra size is cheap for a single important photo.

Step 4: Convert the Photos

Click the Convert button. The decoder reads each HEIC file, renders the photo, and exports a JPG.

A standard 12-megapixel photo takes a second or two. A 48-megapixel photo takes a little longer, since there is far more data to decode.

Each card updates as it finishes, showing the new JPG size next to the original HEIC size. The JPG is usually larger, and that is normal.

If a file fails, read the error message. A corrupted file or a misnamed file is the usual cause, and the rest of the batch keeps converting.

Step 5: Preview Before Downloading

Click Compare on any card to see the original and the converted JPG side by side. Drag the split handle across the photo to inspect it closely.

Check faces first, since they are what people notice. Then check fine detail such as hair, fabric, and text in the background.

Look at the colours too. Display P3 wide-gamut colours shift slightly during conversion, and the preview shows whether the shift matters for that photo.

If a photo looks soft, raise the quality and convert again. The original HEIC stays in the queue, so you never need to re-upload it.

Step 6: Download the JPGs

Click Download on a single card to save one JPG. The filename keeps your original name with a .jpg extension, so it is easy to find.

For several photos, click Download All to get a single ZIP archive with every converted JPG inside. That is far quicker than downloading them one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a JPG in your photo viewer, confirm it looks right, and check that it opens in the app that needs it.

Keep the HEIC originals. They are the best-quality copies you own, and you can always convert them again if you need a different format later.

Converting Directly on Your iPhone

Open the tool in Safari on your iPhone. Tap the upload area, and your photo library opens for you to pick images.

Select the photos you need. They upload into the converter from the library, and the conversion runs on the phone itself.

Downloads go to the Files app. Open Files, tap Downloads, and your JPGs are there, ready to share or attach to a form.

This is the fastest way to send an iPhone photo to a Windows user. Convert on the phone, then share the JPG from Files, and the recipient can open it immediately.

Converting a Whole Album at Once

Drop an entire album into the converter instead of converting one photo at a time. Every HEIC converts with the same settings, and the ZIP holds them all.

Watch for error badges while the album converts. One corrupted file shows a warning without stopping the rest, so the good photos still finish.

Use the batch summary to see the total size. A vacation album of two hundred photos may produce a large ZIP, so check the total before downloading.

Sort the album first if it mixes photos and screenshots. Screenshots convert well at 90 percent too, but they may need different handling afterwards.

After Converting: What to Do Next

If the JPGs are for a form, check the size limit before uploading. A converted JPG at 90 percent quality may exceed a 2MB cap, and you may need to compress it.

Use the 100KB compressor or the target file size compressor when a portal demands an exact size. Convert first, then compress, in that order.

If the JPGs are for a website, consider converting them to WebP instead. The JPG to WebP converter takes your new JPGs and shrinks them for the web.

If the JPGs are for printing, keep them at 90 percent or higher. Print reveals compression flaws, and a high-quality JPG is the safest choice.

Fixing Common Problems

If a file fails with an invalid HEIC error, confirm it is a real HEIC photo. Open it on your iPhone first; if it will not open there either, the file is corrupted.

If conversion is slow, the photo is probably a 48-megapixel capture. Give it a few extra seconds, since decoding that much data takes time on any device.

If the JPG looks dull, the colour shift is the cause. Display P3 colours convert to standard colours, and a small saturation boost in any photo app restores the look.

If a Live Photo loses its motion, that is expected. The converter exports the still frame as a JPG, and the video part is discarded by design.

Stopping HEIC in Future Photos

On your iPhone, open Settings, then Camera, then Formats. Choose Most Compatible, and future photos will save as JPG instead of HEIC.

The trade-off is storage. JPG photos take roughly twice the space of HEIC photos, so your camera roll fills up faster.

Most people are better off leaving HEIC on and converting when needed. The space savings are real, and the conversion takes seconds.

If you share photos with Windows users every day, switching to JPG saves everyone the conversion step. Decide based on who receives your photos most often.

Converting Photos Received in Messages

Photos often arrive in messaging apps as HEIC files, and they refuse to open on your computer. A friend sends a photo from their iPhone, and your Windows laptop shows a broken icon.

Save the photo to your device first, then drop it into the converter. Converting straight from the messaging app rarely works, since the file must exist on your device.

Check the photo quality after saving. Messaging apps often compress images automatically, so the saved file may already be lower quality than the original photo.

After converting, share the JPG back to the chat if others need it. A JPG opens for everyone in the group, while the original HEIC fails for half of them.

Keep the original message as a backup. If the conversion goes wrong, you can download the photo again and try different settings without asking the sender to resend it.

Converting HEIC Files on Windows

Windows does not decode HEIC by default, which makes the browser converter the easiest path. Open the tool in Chrome or Edge, and the decoding runs inside the tab.

You do not need to install the HEIF codec from the Microsoft Store. The converter brings its own decoder, so Windows itself never needs to understand HEIC.

Drag the HEIC files from File Explorer onto the upload area. You can select many files at once, and the batch converts with one click.

After converting, the JPGs open in Windows Photos immediately. They also attach to emails, upload to forms, and print without any further steps.

For a large collection, convert in batches of fifty. The browser handles each batch smoothly, and a failure in one batch does not affect the others.

Converting HEIC for a Job Application

Job portals list JPG and PNG as accepted formats, and a HEIC photo bounces straight back. Convert before uploading, and check the portal rules first.

Convert the photo to JPG at 90 percent quality. The face stays sharp, the colours stay natural, and the file looks professional to the recruiter.

Check the file size limit after converting. A portal that caps uploads at 2MB may reject a 4MB JPG, and you may need to compress it with the 100KB or 200KB tool.

Check the dimensions too. A portal that demands a specific pixel size will reject a photo that is the right format but the wrong shape.

Upload the converted photo early, before the deadline. A last-minute conversion leaves no time to fix a problem, while an early upload leaves room to try again.

Keeping Converted Copies Organised

Converted JPGs deserve clear names, since a folder of IMG_4821.jpg files is useless a month later. Keep the original name and change the extension, so the file stays recognisable.

Store the converted copies in a folder named for their purpose, such as job-application or family-share. A purpose-named folder is easy to find when you need the file again.

Keep the HEIC originals in a separate archive folder. They are the best-quality copies, and a clear separation prevents the two sets from getting mixed up.

Delete failed or duplicate conversions promptly. A folder full of half-finished attempts slows you down every time you search it, while a tidy folder makes the next job faster.

Back up the originals before any large conversion job. A converted file can never become a perfect original again, so the masters deserve a safe copy.

Converting HEIC for Social Media

Social media platforms accept HEIC uploads, but they convert them to JPG or WebP anyway. Uploading a JPG directly gives you control over the quality, instead of trusting the platform's conversion.

Convert the photo to JPG at 90 percent quality before uploading. The face stays sharp, the colours stay natural, and the platform's compression has a good starting point.

Check the platform's preferred dimensions before converting. Instagram, Facebook, and X each have ideal sizes, and the right size matters more than the format.

For a profile picture, a square crop works best. Crop the photo to a square before converting, so the platform does not crop it for you in an awkward place.

Download the platform's version after uploading and check it. A quick look at the published post confirms the photo survived the platform's processing intact.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert HEIC to JPG on my iPhone without a computer?

Open the converter in Safari on your iPhone, tap the upload area, and pick photos from your library. The conversion runs on the phone, and the JPGs download to your Files app. No computer, app install, or account is needed.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG in bulk on Windows?

Yes. Open the tool in Chrome or Edge on Windows, drag in as many HEIC files as you like, and download the whole batch as one ZIP. The decoding runs in the browser, so Windows never needs its own HEIC support.

Why does my converted JPG look bigger in megabytes than the HEIC?

HEIC is a more efficient format than JPG, so the same photo needs more bytes as a JPG. The picture itself is unchanged, only the storage is less efficient. The larger size is the price of a format that opens everywhere.

What happens to Live Photos when I convert HEIC to JPG?

The converter exports the still key photo as a JPG and discards the short video clip. That is correct for forms, websites, and printing, which need a still image. If you want to keep the motion, share the Live Photo from the Photos app instead.

Can I convert HEIC to JPG without losing the photo date?

The converted JPG loses its EXIF metadata, including the original date, because drawing onto a canvas strips it. If the date matters, note it before converting, or rename the JPG with the date in the filename after downloading.

Is there a way to convert HEIC files that failed with an error?

First confirm the file is a real HEIC by opening it on your iPhone. If it opens there, try converting it alone rather than in a batch. If it fails everywhere, the file is likely corrupted, and you need the original photo from the iPhone again.
