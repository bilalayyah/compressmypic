How to Convert an Image to PDF

Turn one photo or a whole set into a PDF in minutes. Upload, order the pages, pick a size, and download your document.

Converting an image to PDF is one of the most useful everyday file jobs. A photo becomes a document, a set of scans becomes a single file, and everything is ready to send or print.

The whole process takes a few minutes, even for a long document. You upload the images, arrange them, choose a page size, and download the finished PDF.

This guide walks through each step in order, so your first PDF comes out clean and professional.

Step 0: Gather and Prepare Your Images

Collect every image that belongs in the document before you open the tool. A passport application, for example, might need a photo, a signature, and a scanned form.

Check that each image is the right way up. A sideways scan becomes a sideways page, so rotate any crooked images first with the rotate tool.

Use the best-quality originals you have. A blurry scan will not sharpen inside a PDF, and a low-resolution photo will look worse when printed.

Files up to 20MB each are accepted. Most phone photos and scans are well under that limit, so you rarely need to shrink anything first.

Step 1: Open the Image to PDF Tool

Open the Image to PDF tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS, with nothing to install.

You will see an upload area and the document settings below it. The settings stay visible while you work, so you can adjust them at any point before generating.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The PDF is built on your device, which matters when the images are ID documents or receipts.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick photos from your gallery.

Step 2: Upload Images and Set the Order

Drag your images onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can also paste screenshots directly with Ctrl+V, which is handy for quick documents.

The images appear in the Page Order list in the sequence you added them. Drag any page up or down to rearrange it until the order is correct.

You can add more images later, even after inspecting the list. New images join the end, and you drag them into place like the rest.

Remove any duplicate or unwanted pages now. It is much easier to fix the list before generating than to rebuild the PDF afterwards.

Step 3: Choose a Page Size

Pick A4 for most documents. It is the standard page size in most of the world, and official systems expect it for forms, applications, and scanned paperwork.

Pick US Letter if the recipient is in the United States or Canada, or if the form specifically asks for Letter size. The difference is small but visible when printed.

Pick Fit to Image Size when the PDF is a photo collection. Each page matches its image exactly, with no white space and no cropping, which suits albums and scan archives.

If you are unsure, A4 is the safe default. It prints correctly almost everywhere and looks professional on screen.

Step 4: Set Orientation and Placement

Leave Orientation on Auto unless you have a reason to change it. Auto reads each image and gives portrait photos a portrait page and landscape photos a landscape page.

If every image is the same shape, you can force Portrait or Landscape for a uniform document. Uniform pages look tidier for formal submissions.

For Placement, choose Contain for documents. Contain fits the whole image inside the page, so no signature or corner is ever cropped off.

Choose Cover only for photo collections, where a full-bleed page looks better. Cover crops the overflow, so never use it for IDs or forms.

Step 5: Set Margins and Quality

Pick a margin that suits the purpose. No Margin suits full-page photos, Small Margin suits general documents, and Medium Margin suits anything you will print and file.

Remember that printers cannot print to the very edge of the paper. A marginless PDF may lose a sliver of the image on paper, so use at least a small margin for print.

Set the quality slider to high for text and ID photos. Small print blurs quickly at low quality, and a blurry document can be rejected.

For photo albums meant for screens, medium quality is fine. The images still look clean, and the PDF stays small enough to send by email.

Step 6: Preview and Check Everything

Look through the Page Order list one more time. Check the sequence, the orientation of each page, and that no page is upside down.

Confirm the page size and margins match the purpose. A form that needs A4 with margins should not arrive as Fit to Image with no margins.

Check the filename setting. Give the PDF a clear name, such as visa-documents.pdf, so the recipient knows what it is without opening it.

This is your last chance to fix things cheaply. A minute of checking now saves a rebuild later.

Step 7: Generate and Download the PDF

Click the Generate button. A progress message shows Encoding page X of Y, so you can watch the document build page by page.

Generation takes a few seconds for a short document and a little longer for a long one. Twenty pages usually finish in under half a minute.

When the download dialog appears, save the file. If the dialog disappears before you notice, check your Downloads folder, since the file is saved there automatically.

Open the finished PDF and look through it once. Confirm the page count, the order, and that the smallest text is readable.

Converting a Single Photo to PDF

A single photo becomes a one-page PDF with the same steps. Upload the photo, pick a page size, and generate.

For a single photo, Fit to Image Size is often the best choice. The page matches the photo exactly, with no white borders and no wasted space.

A single-photo PDF is useful for sending a picture to someone who needs a document format. Some forms accept only PDF uploads, and this is the quickest way to comply.

Name the file clearly, such as profile-photo.pdf, so the recipient recognises it immediately.

Converting Scanned Documents

Scans are the most common PDF job. A multi-page form, a contract, or a set of receipts becomes one tidy document.

Use A4 page size and Contain placement for scans. That combination keeps every word visible and every page the same size.

Keep the quality slider high for scans. Small print and signatures blur at low quality, and a blurry scan can be rejected by the receiving office.

Scan at 200 DPI or higher if you control the scanner. A low-resolution scan cannot be sharpened later, no matter what quality you choose in the PDF.

Fixing Common Problems

If a page comes out sideways, the source image was sideways. Rotate the image before adding it, or remove it, rotate it, and add it again.

If the PDF is too large to email, lower the quality slider one step and generate again. Photos compress well at medium quality, while scans need a gentler touch.

If a page is cropped, you probably chose Cover placement. Switch to Contain and generate again, and the whole image will fit.

If the download dialog vanished, check the Downloads folder. The file is there even if you closed the dialog, so you do not need to generate again.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick photos from your gallery. You can select several photos at once, and they arrive in the order you picked them.

Drag pages in the order list with your finger to rearrange them. The list is touch-friendly, and a long press usually starts the drag.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, open the Files app; on Android, open your file manager.

For a long document, use a desktop if you can. The bigger screen makes ordering twenty pages far easier than a phone does.

Converting Receipts for Expenses

Expense claims need receipts, and a PDF of receipts is far tidier than a folder of photos. One document per trip or month keeps the finance team happy.

Photograph each receipt flat, with good light and no shadows. A crooked or shadowed receipt is hard to read, and the finance team may reject it.

Use A4 page size with Contain placement, so every receipt fits fully on its page. A cropped total or a missing date is the most common reason for a rejected claim.

Keep the quality slider high for receipts. Small print, such as tax numbers and itemised lines, blurs quickly at low quality, and the claim needs those details.

Name the PDF by month and purpose, such as expenses-november-2026.pdf. A clear name helps the finance team file it correctly and find it again later.

Building a Portfolio PDF

A portfolio PDF shows your best work in a single document that anyone can open. Designers, photographers, and freelancers all use one to win clients.

Choose your strongest images first, then arrange them in a deliberate order. The first page should be your best work, since it sets the impression for everything after it.

Use Fit to Image Size for a portfolio, so each image fills its page without white borders. The full-bleed layout feels deliberate and professional, like a printed book.

Keep the file size reasonable. A portfolio that is too large to email is useless, so balance quality against size, and aim for under 10MB if you can.

Include a final page with your contact details. A client who loves the work should find your email and phone number without hunting for them.

Combining Photos and Scans in One PDF

Real documents often mix photos and scans. A visa application might contain a JPG photo, a PNG scan of a passport, and a JPG scan of a bank statement.

The PDF builder accepts mixed formats in one document. Each page keeps its own source format, so you do not need to convert everything to one format first.

Order the pages logically, with identity documents first and supporting evidence after. A clear order helps the reader follow the story of the application.

Check the mixed pages carefully after generating. A photo page and a scan page may need different quality settings, and the single slider is a compromise for both.

If one page looks wrong, adjust the source image rather than the PDF settings. Convert a problematic photo to the right format first, then rebuild the PDF.

Sending the PDF to Someone Else

Before sending, open the finished PDF and read it as the recipient would. Check the page count, the order, and that the smallest text is readable.

For email, attach the PDF directly if it is under the size limit. Most services allow 20MB to 25MB, and a well-built PDF is usually far smaller than that.

For larger PDFs, upload to cloud storage and share a link. The recipient downloads the file without any size problem, and you can revoke the link later if needed.

For messaging apps, check that the PDF sends as a document rather than as images. WhatsApp and Telegram both support PDF attachments, which preserve the layout.

After sending, keep a copy in your sent folder. If the recipient loses it or asks for a different version, you can resend or rebuild without starting over.

Converting ID Photos for Applications

Application forms often need an ID photo as a PDF page, and the photo must be clear. A blurry ID photo can delay an application by weeks, so the conversion deserves care.

Use a recent, well-lit photo with a plain background. The face should be front-facing, with no shadows and no sunglasses, following the application's photo rules.

Choose A4 page size with Contain placement, so the whole photo fits on the page. A cropped chin or forehead is the most common reason for a rejected ID photo.

Keep the quality slider high for ID photos. Small details such as the eyes and the mouth must stay sharp, since the application system checks them.

Check the file size after generating. A portal that caps uploads at 2MB may reject a large PDF, and you may need to lower the quality one step.

Frequently asked questions

Can I convert a single image to PDF, or do I need several?

A single image works fine and becomes a one-page PDF. The tool is built for batches, but there is no minimum count. For one photo, Fit to Image Size usually gives the cleanest result, since the page matches the photo exactly.

What happens if I upload images in the wrong order?

You can fix the order before generating. The Page Order list lets you drag any page up or down until the sequence is right. You can also add or remove images at that stage. Once the order looks correct, generate the PDF.

Can I convert images to PDF on my phone?

Yes. Open the tool in your phone browser, tap the upload area, and pick photos from your gallery. All settings work with touch, and the finished PDF downloads to your phone. For long documents, a desktop is easier, but a phone handles short ones well.

Will the PDF keep the original image quality?

Mostly, yes, but the quality slider matters. At high quality, the images inside the PDF are nearly identical to the originals. At low quality, they are compressed and may look softer. For documents with text, keep the quality high.

Can I password-protect the PDF I create?

Not with this tool, which builds a standard PDF without encryption. If you need a password, open the finished PDF in a PDF app that supports protection and set one there. The generated file itself is a normal, unprotected PDF.

Does converting images to PDF reduce their file size?

It can, depending on the quality slider. At medium quality, the images inside the PDF are compressed, so the total file is often smaller than the separate originals. At high quality, the PDF may be similar in size or slightly larger, because of the PDF structure itself.
