How to Convert JPG to PNG

Convert JPG images to PNG in seconds, one file or a batch of twenty. Upload, convert, compare, and download — all in your browser.

Converting a JPG to PNG is a simple job, but it is worth doing correctly. A proper conversion rewrites the file as a genuine PNG, not just a renamed JPG.

The process takes seconds per file, and you can convert up to twenty images at once. Everything runs in your browser, with nothing to install.

This guide walks through each step, and explains what to check before you use the result.

Before You Start: Know Why You Are Converting

Ask yourself what the PNG is for. Editing, design work, and text overlays are good reasons. Making a photo sharper or smaller are not, since PNG does neither.

Check that PNG is actually required. Some tools demand it, but many accept JPG or WebP, which are far smaller for photos.

Find the best JPG original you have. The PNG will preserve it exactly, flaws included, so start from the highest-quality copy.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers high-resolution camera exports and wide digital artwork without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the JPG to PNG Tool

Open the JPG to PNG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS.

You will see an upload area and a convert button. There is no quality slider, since PNG is lossless and there is no quality level to choose.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for personal photos and scanned documents.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick a photo from your gallery.

Step 2: Upload Your JPG Files

Drag your JPG files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. Both .jpg and .jpeg extensions are accepted.

You can upload up to twenty files at once. Each appears as a card with its name, dimensions, and size, so you can confirm the right files loaded.

Check the dimensions on each card. The PNG will keep the exact pixel width and height of the JPG, so a wrong file is easy to spot here.

You can also paste a copied image with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when the JPG is in your clipboard from another app.

Step 3: Convert the Files

Click the Convert button. The tool decodes each JPG, draws it onto a canvas, and encodes it as a PNG.

Conversion is fast, usually a second or two per file. Larger files take a little longer, since there are more pixels to encode losslessly.

Each card updates with the new PNG size next to the old JPG size. Expect the PNG to be much larger, often five to ten times the JPG size for photos.

If a file fails, read the error message. A corrupted JPG or a misnamed file is the usual cause, and the rest of the batch keeps converting.

Step 4: Compare the Result

Click Compare on a card to open the side-by-side viewer. It shows the JPG and the PNG next to each other, so you can confirm zero colour shifting.

Look closely at edges and text. The PNG should look identical to the JPG, since the conversion preserves every pixel exactly.

Check the dimensions in the viewer as well. The width and height should match the original JPG precisely, with no cropping or scaling.

If anything looks different, something went wrong. Re-upload the original JPG and convert again, rather than trying to fix the PNG.

Step 5: Download the PNG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one PNG. The filename keeps your original name with a .png extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted PNG inside. That is far quicker than downloading twenty files one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a PNG in your photo viewer and confirm it looks right and opens in the app that needs it.

Keep the original JPGs as well. The PNG is a working copy, and the JPG remains the smaller, more portable version of the same image.

Converting a Batch of Twenty

Select up to twenty JPGs in the file picker at once. They all load into the queue, and you convert them together with one click.

Watch the Change column as the batch runs. It shows the file size growth cleanly, so you can see which files grew the most.

Photos grow the most, since their texture compresses poorly as PNG. Simple scans and graphics grow far less, because their flat areas compress well.

Download the batch as a ZIP and unzip it into a fresh folder. Mixing PNGs with the original JPGs in one folder causes confusion later.

Checking the File Size Before You Send It

Always check the PNG size against any limit. A form that caps uploads at 5MB may reject a PNG that started as a 1MB JPG.

If the PNG is too large, reconsider whether PNG is needed. A WebP file is often a better compromise, offering lossless quality at a smaller size.

For email attachments, a large PNG batch can exceed the limit. Send the JPGs instead, or compress the PNGs after converting, if the recipient accepts them.

The Change column on each card shows the growth clearly. Use it to decide whether the conversion was worth the extra bytes.

Using the PNG for Editing

Open the PNG in your editor of choice. Photoshop, Figma, Canva, and PowerPoint all accept PNG natively, and the lossless format survives repeated saves.

Add your text, annotations, or overlays to the PNG version. Each save keeps the image exactly as sharp as the last, with no accumulating damage.

Export the finished result in the format the destination needs. A web page wants WebP or JPG, while a print shop may want the PNG itself.

Keep the PNG master after exporting. If you need another export later, you can make it from the PNG without reopening the original JPG.

Fixing Common Problems

If the PNG comes out much larger than expected, that is normal for photos. PNG stores every pixel exactly, and a detailed photo needs many bytes to do that.

If the colours look shifted, the source JPG may have used CMYK print colours. Convert an RGB export instead, or check the result carefully before using it.

If a file fails to convert, check that it is a real JPG. A file named .jpg that is actually a WebP or PNG will confuse the decoder, and renaming it correctly fixes the problem.

If the orientation looks wrong, the JPG's orientation data may be unusual. Open the JPG in a photo viewer first, confirm it displays correctly there, and then convert.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick JPGs from your gallery. The conversion runs on the phone, and the PNGs download to your device.

Keep phone batches small, since large PNGs take time to encode on a mobile processor. Five files at a time is comfortable; twenty is slow.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large batch, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make twenty files far easier to manage.

Converting Old Family Photos

Old family photos are often scanned as JPGs, and converting them to PNG creates a lossless working copy for restoration. The PNG preserves the scan exactly, flaws included, without adding further damage.

Start from the best scan you have. A high-resolution scan at 600 DPI captures the detail that restoration needs, while a low-resolution scan cannot be improved by any format.

Convert the scan to PNG once, at the start of the restoration. All edits, from dust removal to colour correction, happen on the PNG version, which survives repeated saves.

Export the restored result as JPG for sharing. The family gets a small file that opens everywhere, while the PNG master stays in the archive for future work.

Store the PNG masters with clear names and dates. A name such as grandma-wedding-1962-restored.png is useful decades later, while a name such as scan001.png is not.

Converting Screenshots for a Manual

A user manual is full of screenshots, and those screenshots must stay readable through many rounds of editing. Converting them to PNG at the start protects the text.

Take the screenshots at the highest resolution available. A retina screenshot has twice the pixels, and the text stays sharp even after the manual is printed.

Convert each screenshot to PNG before adding annotations. Arrows, circles, and labels added to a PNG stay crisp, while the same annotations on a JPG soften with each save.

Keep the PNG screenshots in a folder per chapter. A chapter folder with numbered images is easy to navigate, and the manual builds itself in order.

Export the finished manual as PDF with the PNG images inside. The text stays sharp in the PDF, and the reader can follow every step without squinting.

Converting Images for a Designer

Designers prefer PNG files, since they open natively in Photoshop, Figma, and Illustrator. A JPG sent to a designer often gets converted to PNG anyway, so sending PNG saves a step.

Convert the images before sending them, and name them clearly. A file called hero-photo.png tells the designer what it is, while IMG_4821.png tells them nothing.

Send the highest-quality versions you have. A designer working from a low-quality JPG cannot produce a sharp result, no matter how skilled they are.

Include the original JPGs as well, in case the designer needs them. A folder with both formats gives the designer options without asking you to resend anything.

Agree on a delivery format before the project starts. A short conversation about JPG versus PNG prevents a folder of wrongly formatted files later.

Converting Product Shots for Editing

Product photos need editing before they go on a shop page. Cropping, background cleanup, and colour correction all work better from a PNG master than from a JPG.

Convert the product JPGs to PNG once, at the start of the editing session. All adjustments happen on the PNG versions, which survive repeated saves without degrading.

Edit one product photo first and agree the look with the team. A single approved example sets the standard for the whole catalogue, so the rest follow the same style.

Export the finished product shots as WebP or JPG for the website. The shop needs small files that load fast, and the PNG masters stay in the archive.

Keep the PNG masters organised by product and date. A clear folder structure makes it easy to find a product shot again when the listing needs updating.

Converting Images for Print

Print work needs the highest quality available, and PNG is the safer format for images with text. A poster, a flyer, or a brochure with sharp lettering prints cleanly from PNG.

Check the resolution before converting. A 1000-pixel image printed at 10 inches is only 100 DPI, which looks soft on paper regardless of the format.

Convert the images to PNG at full resolution, then send them to the print shop. The shop can scale them down if needed, but it cannot add detail that was never there.

Ask the print shop about colour profiles before sending. A PNG with the wrong profile may print with shifted colours, and the shop can advise on the correct setup.

Order a test print for an important job. A single small print confirms the colours and sharpness before you commit to a large order.

Converting Images for a School Assignment

A school assignment often needs images from the web, and those images are usually JPG. Converting them to PNG gives a lossless working copy for cropping and annotation.

Convert the images to PNG before adding them to the assignment. The PNGs survive repeated edits, while each JPG save adds a little damage to the text and diagrams.

For an assignment with many images, convert the whole set at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the PNGs are ready to drop into the document.

Check the images after converting. A quick look at each one confirms it displays correctly, so the assignment has no broken images on submission day.

Cite the image sources in the assignment. A converted image still belongs to its creator, and a proper credit shows good academic practice.

Frequently asked questions

How many JPG files can I convert to PNG at once?

You can convert up to twenty JPG files in one batch and download them as a single ZIP. Each file converts independently, so one bad file does not stop the rest. For larger sets, run several batches of twenty.

Can I convert a JPG to PNG without the file getting bigger?

No, not really. PNG is lossless, so it must store every pixel exactly, and a photo converted from JPG almost always grows several times larger. If file size matters, use WebP instead, which keeps quality high at a much smaller size.

Does JPG to PNG conversion work offline?

Once the page has loaded in your browser, the conversion runs locally and does not need the internet. You need a connection only to open the page the first time. After that, you can convert JPGs offline until you close the tab.

Will my PNG open in Photoshop and Canva?

Yes. PNG is a universal format for design tools, and it opens in Photoshop, Illustrator, Figma, Canva, PowerPoint, and every modern browser. The converted PNG is a standard file with no special requirements.

What should I do if the PNG is too large to upload?

First check whether PNG is actually needed. If the destination accepts WebP or JPG, convert to one of those instead for a far smaller file. If PNG is required, reduce the image dimensions with the resizer before converting, since fewer pixels mean a smaller PNG.

Can I convert a JPG screenshot to PNG for annotation?

Yes, and that is one of the best uses of this tool. Screenshots converted to PNG stay sharp through many rounds of annotation, cropping, and re-saving. Add your arrows and text to the PNG version, then export the finished result for sharing.
