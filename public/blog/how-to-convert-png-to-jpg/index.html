How to Convert PNG to JPG and Shrink It

Convert PNG screenshots and images to JPG in six steps. Pick a matte colour, set quality, preview, and download — one file or a batch.

A PNG screenshot can weigh ten times more than the same image as a JPG. Converting it takes less than a minute, and the result is far easier to send, upload, and store.

The only tricky part is transparency. JPG cannot store see-through pixels, so you need to pick a fill colour for them before converting.

This guide walks through each step, so your first conversion comes out clean.

Before You Start: Check for Transparency

Open the PNG and look at its background. A photo or a screenshot has no transparency, so you can convert without thinking about fill colours.

A logo, an icon, or a product cutout probably has transparency. The see-through areas will need a matte colour, and you should decide which one before you begin.

White suits most destinations. Documents, web pages, and forms usually sit on white, so a white matte looks natural there.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers Retina screenshots and large product renders without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the PNG to JPG Tool

Open the PNG to JPG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS, with nothing to install.

You will see an upload area, a matte colour selector, and a quality slider. Those three controls are the whole job.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for screenshots of private dashboards and documents.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick PNGs from your gallery.

Step 2: Upload Your PNG Files

Drag your PNG files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select many files at once for a batch.

Each file appears as a card with a thumbnail, its dimensions, and its size. A quick scan confirms the right files loaded and nothing extra slipped in.

You can also paste a screenshot straight from your clipboard with Ctrl+V. That is the fastest way to convert a screenshot you just took.

Interlaced PNGs are supported, including the Adam7 format. The decoder reads them normally, so you do not need to convert them first.

Step 3: Pick the Matte Colour

Click the matte colour selector to choose the fill for transparent areas. White is the default and suits most uses.

Pick dark charcoal if the image will sit on a dark background, or enter a hex code to match a specific website brand colour exactly.

If your PNG has no transparency, this setting does nothing. Photos and screenshots pass through unchanged, whatever colour you pick.

Check a logo card after converting to see how the matte looks. A wrong colour is obvious immediately, and you can change it and convert again.

Step 4: Set the Quality Slider

The quality slider runs from 20 to 100 percent. For screenshots with text, 85 to 90 percent keeps the letters crisp.

For photographs saved as PNG, 80 to 85 percent is the sweet spot. The photo looks clean, and the file shrinks dramatically.

Lower the quality only when a size limit forces it. A drop from 90 to 80 percent often halves the file size with little visible change.

Do not go below 70 percent for screenshots. Small text blurs quickly at low quality, and a blurry screenshot is worse than a large one.

Step 5: Convert and Preview the Result

Click the Convert button. Each file converts independently, so you can watch progress card by card.

Each card shows the new JPG size next to the old PNG size. A good conversion shrinks a screenshot by 80 percent or more.

Click Compare on a card to inspect the result side by side. Drag the split handle across the image and zoom into text, edges, and shadows.

If the text looks soft, raise the quality by five points and convert again. The original PNG stays in the queue, so you never re-upload.

Step 6: Download the JPG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one JPG. The filename keeps your original name with a .jpg extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted JPG inside. The ZIP name tells you it contains the converted files.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a JPG in your photo viewer, confirm it looks right, and check that it opens where it needs to.

Keep the original PNGs as well. A JPG can never become a perfect PNG again, so the originals are your safety net.

Converting Screenshots the Right Way

Screenshots are the most common PNG to JPG job. Mac and Windows both save screenshots as PNG, and those files are far larger than they need to be.

Paste the screenshot directly with Ctrl+V for the fastest workflow. Take the screenshot, paste it into the converter, and download the JPG seconds later.

Keep the quality at 85 percent or higher for screenshots. Menus, buttons, and body text all need that quality to stay readable.

For a screenshot that must stay pixel-perfect, such as legal evidence, consider WebP instead. It preserves text better than JPG at small sizes.

Converting a Batch of Screenshots

Select up to 25 screenshots at once in the file picker. They all load into the queue, and one click converts them all.

Use the same matte colour and quality for the whole batch. Consistency matters when the screenshots belong to the same document or presentation.

Watch for error badges as the batch runs. A single corrupted file shows a warning without stopping the rest, so the good files still finish.

Download the batch as a ZIP and unzip it into a fresh folder. Mixing converted JPGs with the original PNGs in one folder causes confusion later.

Fixing Common Problems

If a transparent logo gets a black box, the matte colour was black or unset. Change it to white or your page colour and convert again.

If the text looks blurry, the quality was too low. Raise the slider to 90 percent and convert again from the original PNG.

If the JPG is barely smaller than the PNG, the image is probably a simple graphic. PNG already compresses flat colours well, so the conversion saves little.

If a file fails to convert, check that it is a real PNG. A file named .png that is actually a WebP will confuse the decoder, and renaming it correctly fixes the problem.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick PNGs from your gallery. Screenshots saved on your phone convert the same way as desktop ones.

Keep phone batches small, since large PNGs take time to encode on a mobile processor. Ten files at a time is comfortable.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large batch of screenshots, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make twenty-five files far easier to manage.

Converting Mac Screenshots

Mac screenshots save as PNG by default, and they are large. A retina screenshot can weigh 5MB, while the same image as a JPG weighs 500KB, with no visible difference.

Take the screenshot with Shift, Command, and 4, then drag the file into the converter. The PNG converts to JPG in seconds, and the result is ready to share.

Keep the quality at 85 percent or higher for Mac screenshots. The retina text stays crisp, and the file shrinks by 80 percent or more.

For a screenshot that must stay pixel-perfect, such as a design review, keep the PNG instead. The larger file is worth it when every pixel matters.

Change the Mac screenshot format to JPG in Terminal if you take them often. A single command makes every future screenshot a JPG, removing the conversion step entirely.

Converting Windows Screenshots

Windows screenshots save as PNG too, whether taken with Print Screen or the Snipping Tool. Those files are large, and converting them to JPG saves real space.

Paste the screenshot directly into the converter with Ctrl+V. The clipboard image loads immediately, and the JPG downloads seconds later, without any file juggling.

For Snipping Tool captures, save the snip first, then convert the file. The snip tool saves PNG by default, and the converter handles the rest.

Keep the quality at 85 percent for Windows screenshots. Menus, taskbars, and dialog boxes all stay readable, and the file is a fraction of the PNG size.

For a screenshot of a full desktop with a photo wallpaper, the saving is dramatic. The wallpaper compresses beautifully as JPG, while PNG stores every pixel of it exactly.

Converting Phone Screenshots

Phone screenshots save as PNG on both iPhone and Android, and they pile up quickly. A phone with a hundred screenshots can waste a gigabyte on PNG files alone.

Convert the screenshots you need to keep, and delete the rest. Most screenshots are temporary, and keeping them all wastes storage for no benefit.

For a screenshot you must keep, such as a receipt or a confirmation, convert it to JPG at 85 percent quality. The text stays readable, and the file is far smaller.

Share the JPG directly from your phone after converting. It uploads faster, uses less mobile data, and opens on any device the recipient uses.

Check your phone storage after a bulk conversion. The freed space is often surprising, and the phone runs better with room to breathe.

Converting Design Mockups

Design mockups are often exported as PNG, since the format keeps text and shapes sharp. Those files are large, and converting them to JPG makes them easier to share with clients.

For a mockup going to a client for review, convert to JPG at 90 percent quality. The design stays crisp, the file emails easily, and the client can open it anywhere.

For a mockup that the client will annotate, keep the PNG instead. The lossless format survives the annotation rounds without degrading, while JPG softens with each save.

Export the mockup at twice the display size before converting. A larger source produces a sharper JPG, since the encoder has more detail to work with.

Keep the PNG masters in the project folder after converting. If the client asks for changes, the masters are ready, and the JPGs are just delivery copies.

Converting Images for a Presentation

Presentation images need to be sharp and reasonably sized. A PNG screenshot in a slide looks crisp, while a JPG photo keeps the deck from becoming enormous.

Convert screenshots to JPG at 90 percent quality for slides. The text stays readable on a projector, and the file is small enough to keep the deck manageable.

For a full-screen photo on a slide, JPG at 85 percent is the right choice. The photo looks clean, the slide loads fast, and the audience sees the image, not the pixels.

Check the total deck size after converting. A presentation over 50MB may struggle to email or upload, so converting the images is often the fix.

Test the presentation on the projector before the talk. A slide that looks sharp on a laptop may look soft when projected, and a quick test catches the problem early.

Converting Images for a WhatsApp Group

WhatsApp compresses images automatically, so a PNG screenshot sent to a group arrives as a small JPG anyway. Converting it yourself first gives you control over the quality.

Convert the screenshot to JPG at 85 percent quality before sending it. The text stays readable, the file sends fast, and WhatsApp's compression has a good starting point.

For an image that must stay sharp, such as a payment confirmation, send it as a document instead. A document attachment skips WhatsApp's compression entirely.

Check the image after sending it. Open it in the chat and confirm the text is readable, since a blurry screenshot in a group chat helps nobody.

Keep the original PNG on your phone after sending. If someone asks for a sharper version, the PNG is ready, and you do not need to retake the screenshot.

Frequently asked questions

What matte colour should I pick for a PNG with transparency?

Match the background where the JPG will appear. White suits most documents, web pages, and forms. Dark charcoal suits dark designs, and a hex code matches a specific brand colour exactly. Check the converted logo on its destination background before using it.

Can I convert a PNG screenshot to JPG without blurring the text?

Yes, if you keep the quality at 85 percent or higher. Small text is the first thing to suffer from JPG compression, so avoid low settings for screenshots. Use the Compare viewer to zoom into the text before downloading, and raise the quality if the letters look soft.

How many PNG files can I convert at once?

You can convert a batch of 25 PNG screenshots in one pass and download them as a single ZIP. Each file converts independently, so one bad file does not stop the rest. For larger sets, run several batches.

Why is my converted JPG bigger than the original PNG?

That happens with tiny, simple icons, where JPG adds overhead that PNG does not need. A 20KB flat monochrome icon can grow slightly as a JPG. For simple graphics, keep the PNG. JPG only saves space on detailed images such as photos and screenshots.

Does converting PNG to JPG remove the background?

No, it fills the transparent background with the matte colour you choose. The result is a JPG with a solid background, not a transparent one. To remove a background properly, use the background remover tool and save the result as PNG.

Can I change the matte colour after converting?

Yes. Pick a new matte colour and convert again from the original PNG, which stays in the queue. You do not need to re-upload. Check the new result with Compare before downloading, since the matte colour is the most visible setting in the whole conversion.
