How to Convert SVG to PNG

Convert SVG logos and icons to PNG at the size you need. Upload, pick a scale, check the result, and download — one file or a whole pack.

An SVG logo is infinitely scalable, but many places still want a PNG. Converting it takes seconds, and the only decision that matters is the output size.

Pick the right scale, and the PNG looks perfect. Pick the wrong one, and it comes out blurry or unnecessarily huge.

This guide walks through each step, from uploading the SVG to downloading a sharp PNG.

Before You Start: Prepare the SVG

Open the SVG in a browser first and confirm it renders correctly. If it looks wrong there, it will look wrong as a PNG too.

Convert text to outlines in your design tool if the SVG contains lettering. Outlined text becomes shapes, so it renders exactly regardless of fonts.

Embed any linked images as data URIs inside the SVG. The converter cannot fetch external files, so a linked photo will be missing from the PNG.

Remove any background rectangle if you want a transparent PNG. A background shape becomes part of the image, while a transparent SVG stays transparent.

Step 1: Open the SVG to PNG Tool

Open the SVG to PNG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS.

You will see an upload area and a scale selector. Those two controls are the whole job.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for client logos and brand assets.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick SVG files from your device.

Step 2: Upload Your SVG Files

Drag your SVG files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select many files at once for a batch.

Each file appears as a card with a thumbnail, so you can confirm the right files loaded. A wrong file is easy to spot and remove.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted, which covers even huge illustrated files with thousands of paths. Very complex SVGs simply take a second or two longer.

You can also paste a copied SVG with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when the SVG code is in your clipboard from another app.

Step 3: Pick the Output Scale

The scale selector offers 1x, 2x, 3x, and 4x. The scale multiplies the SVG's natural size, so a 24-pixel icon at 4x becomes a 96-pixel PNG.

For a website icon, 2x or 3x is usually enough. The PNG looks sharp on retina screens without wasting bytes.

For a logo on a presentation slide, 4x gives a comfortable margin. A small logo scaled up in PowerPoint stays sharp when the PNG has enough pixels.

For print, pick the scale that reaches 300 DPI at the printed size. A logo printed at 2 inches wide needs at least 600 pixels, so work backwards from the print size.

Step 4: Convert the Files

Click the Convert button. The tool draws each SVG onto a canvas at the chosen scale and encodes the result as a PNG.

Conversion is fast, usually a second or two per file. Very complex SVGs with thousands of paths take a little longer to draw.

Each card updates with the new PNG size and dimensions. Check the dimensions to confirm the scale produced the size you expected.

If a file fails with an error, the SVG may contain unsupported features or malformed code. Open it in a browser to confirm it renders there first.

Step 5: Check the Result

Click Compare on a card to see the SVG and the PNG side by side. Zoom into the logo edges and confirm they are sharp and clean.

Check the text first, if the SVG contains any. Wrong-looking text means a missing font, and the fix is to convert the text to outlines and convert again.

Check for missing images. A blank space where a photo should be means the image was linked externally, and you need to embed it as a data URI.

Check the transparency. A logo that should be transparent must show no white box around it, or the SVG had a background rectangle you need to remove.

Step 6: Download the PNG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one PNG. The filename keeps your original name with a .png extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted PNG inside. That is far quicker than downloading them one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a PNG in your photo viewer and confirm it looks sharp at the size you need.

Keep the original SVG files as well. They are the masters, and you can rasterize them again at any size whenever you need a different PNG.

Converting a Whole Icon Pack

Select all the SVG icons at once in the file picker. They load into the queue, and one click converts them all at the same scale.

Use the same scale for the whole pack. Consistency matters when icons appear together, since mixed sizes look uneven.

Check two or three icons after converting, rather than all fifty. If those look good, the rest will too, since the settings are the same.

Upload the PNG icons where they are needed. A favicon, an app icon, or a slide deck all accept PNG files without any further conversion.

Choosing Size for Each Purpose

For a favicon, rasterize at the exact sizes the site needs, usually 16, 32, and 48 pixels. The favicon generator can also make those sizes from a PNG.

For a social media avatar, 400 by 400 pixels is a good size. It looks sharp on desktop and mobile, and the file stays small.

For a presentation logo, 4x the SVG size usually gives enough pixels. A 100-pixel logo at 4x becomes 400 pixels, which looks sharp on a slide.

For print, calculate from the DPI. A 2-inch logo at 300 DPI needs 600 pixels, so pick the scale that reaches at least that number.

Fixing Common Problems

If the PNG looks blurry, the scale was too low. Rasterize again at a higher scale from the SVG original, since a low-resolution PNG cannot be sharpened.

If the text looks wrong, the font is missing. Convert the text to outlines in your design tool and convert again.

If a linked photo is missing, embed it as a data URI inside the SVG. The converter cannot fetch external files, so the image must travel inside the SVG.

If the file fails with an error, open the SVG in a browser. If it renders there, try converting it alone rather than in a batch.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick SVG files from your device. The conversion runs on the phone, and the PNGs download to your device.

Keep phone batches small, since complex SVGs take time to rasterize on a mobile processor. Ten files at a time is comfortable.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large icon pack, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make fifty files far easier to manage.

Converting a Logo for a Business Card

A business card logo must print sharply at a tiny size, so rasterize the SVG at 4x. A small logo with enough pixels behind it stays crisp on the card.

Check the colours after converting, since print colours differ from screen colours. A quick test print confirms the brand colours look right on paper.

Send the PNG to the print shop with the SVG as a backup. The shop can rasterize the SVG itself if the PNG has a problem, so providing both covers every case.

Keep the PNG file small, since a business card printer does not need a huge image. A 1000-pixel logo is plenty for a card, and a larger file only slows the upload.

Order a test card before printing the full batch. A single card confirms the logo size, the colours, and the sharpness before you commit to hundreds.

Converting Icons for a Website

Website icons start as SVG files, and they become PNGs for older browsers. Rasterize at 2x or 3x the display size, so the icons look sharp on retina screens.

Use the same scale for the whole icon set. Consistency matters when icons appear together, since mixed sizes look uneven.

Test the icons on the actual website before publishing them. An icon that looks perfect in the design tool may look different against the site's background colour.

For a modern website, consider using the SVG files directly. Browsers display SVG natively, and the icons scale perfectly without any rasterization at all.

Keep the SVG masters in the project folder after converting. If the website redesigns, the icons can be rasterized again at new sizes without redrawing them.

Converting an SVG for a T-Shirt Print

A T-shirt print needs a high-resolution image, and SVG is the ideal source. Rasterize at the largest scale the tool allows, up to the 8000-pixel cap, for a crisp print.

Check the design after converting, since a T-shirt print shows every flaw. A logo that looks fine on screen may reveal rough edges when printed large on fabric.

Send the PNG to the print shop with the SVG as a backup. The shop's software may prefer one format over the other, and providing both covers every case.

For a dark T-shirt, check the transparency. A logo with a transparent background prints cleanly on dark fabric, while a white-boxed logo looks wrong.

Order a single test shirt before printing a batch. A test print confirms the size, the colours, and the placement before you commit to dozens of shirts.

Converting an SVG for a Presentation

Presentation slides need sharp logos, and SVG is the ideal source. Rasterize at 4x the SVG size, so the logo stays crisp when projected onto a large screen.

Check the logo on the actual slide background after converting. A logo that looks perfect on white may show a faint fringe on a coloured slide if the quality was low.

For a deck with many slides, convert all the logos at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the PNGs are ready to drop into the slides.

Test the deck on the projector before the talk. A slide that looks sharp on a laptop may look soft when projected, and a quick test catches the problem early.

Keep the SVG masters in the project folder after converting. If the presentation is reused with a new template, the logos can be rasterized again at new sizes.

Converting an SVG for a Favicon

A favicon is the tiny icon in the browser tab, and it usually starts as an SVG logo. Rasterize it at 16, 32, and 48 pixels for the standard favicon sizes.

Design the favicon simply, since tiny sizes hide detail. A complex logo at 16 pixels becomes a blur, while a simple shape stays recognisable.

Test the favicon in several browsers after adding it. A quick check confirms it displays correctly, so the site looks polished in every tab.

For modern browsers, consider using the SVG favicon directly. Browsers display SVG favicons natively, and the icon scales perfectly without any rasterization.

Keep the SVG master after converting. If the site redesigns, the favicon can be rasterized again at new sizes without redrawing the logo.

Converting an SVG for a Website Banner

A website banner is a wide image, and SVG is the ideal source when the banner is a graphic rather than a photo. Rasterize at the banner's display width for a sharp result.

Check the banner dimensions before converting. A banner displayed at 1200 pixels wide needs a PNG of at least that width, or it will look soft.

For a banner with text, rasterize at 2x the display size. The text stays sharp on retina screens, and the file is still small enough to load quickly.

Test the banner on desktop and phone after converting. A banner that looks perfect on a wide screen may crop badly on a narrow one, and a quick test catches it.

Keep the SVG master in the project folder. If the website redesigns with a new banner size, the graphic can be rasterized again without redrawing it.

Frequently asked questions

What size is the PNG at 1x scale?

At 1x, the PNG matches the SVG natural size, so a 24px icon becomes a 24px PNG. For sharp results on modern screens, use 2x or 3x, and for presentations or print, use 4x. The export caps at 8000px on the longest side, which rarely matters for icons and logos.

Can I convert SVG files on my phone?

Yes. Open the tool in your phone browser, tap the upload area, and pick SVG files from your device. The conversion runs on the phone, and the PNGs download to your device. Keep phone batches small, since complex SVGs take time to rasterize on mobile.

What filename will the exported PNG keep?

The PNG keeps your original SVG filename with a .png extension, so logo.svg becomes logo.png. That makes it easy to find and keeps your files organised. For a batch, the ZIP contains every PNG with its matching original name.

Does the converter support SVG animations?

No, not really. SVG animations using SMIL or CSS are not preserved, since the converter draws a single static frame onto the canvas. The result is a still PNG of the SVG as it appears at the start. For an animated logo, keep the SVG itself.

Can I rasterize an SVG with an image embedded as a data URI?

Yes. Images embedded as data URIs inside the SVG are part of the file, so they render correctly in the PNG. That is the right way to include photos in an SVG for conversion. Linked external images, by contrast, are left out, since the converter cannot fetch them.

Why does my converted PNG look blurry?

The scale was too low for the size you need. A 24px icon rasterized at 1x is only 24 pixels, and stretching it to 200 pixels makes it blurry. Rasterize again at a higher scale from the SVG original, since a low-resolution PNG cannot be sharpened afterwards.
