How to Convert WebP to JPG

Convert WebP images to JPG in a few steps. Upload, pick a matte colour, set quality, and download — one file or a whole batch.

A WebP image downloaded from a website often refuses to open in the app you need. Converting it to JPG takes less than a minute, and the result works everywhere.

The only tricky part is transparency. JPG cannot store see-through pixels, so you need to pick a fill colour for them before converting.

This guide walks through each step, so your first conversion comes out clean.

Before You Start: Check for Transparency

Open the WebP and look at its background. A photo has no transparency, so you can convert without thinking about fill colours.

A logo, a sticker, or a product cutout probably has transparency. The see-through areas will need a matte colour, and you should decide which one before you begin.

White suits most destinations. Documents, web pages, and forms usually sit on white, so a white matte looks natural there.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers high-resolution downloads and large screenshots without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the WebP to JPG Tool

Open the WebP to JPG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS, with nothing to install.

You will see an upload area, a matte colour selector, and a quality slider. Those three controls are the whole job.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for personal images and downloaded files.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick WebP files from your downloads or gallery.

Step 2: Upload Your WebP Files

Drag your WebP files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select many files at once for a batch.

Each file appears as a card with a thumbnail, its dimensions, and its size. A quick scan confirms the right files loaded and nothing extra slipped in.

Some websites save images without a file extension. Those files still convert, since the tool reads the actual contents rather than trusting the name.

You can also paste a copied image with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when the WebP is in your clipboard from another app.

Step 3: Pick the Matte Colour

Click the matte colour selector to choose the fill for transparent areas. White is the default and suits most uses.

Pick black for dark designs, or enter a hex code to match a specific website background exactly. A matched background makes the logo look like it belongs there.

If your WebP has no transparency, this setting does nothing. Photos pass through unchanged, whatever colour you pick.

For a pure white background behind a logo, the white matte is the right choice. It produces a clean JPG that sits naturally on white pages.

Step 4: Set the Quality Slider

The quality slider controls how much detail the JPG encoder keeps. For most images, 85 percent is the sweet spot.

Use 90 percent or higher for images going to print. Print reveals flaws that screens hide, and the extra size is cheap for a single important image.

For screenshots with text, keep the quality at 85 percent or higher as well. Small letters blur at low quality, and a blurry screenshot looks unprofessional.

Lower the quality to 80 percent only when a size limit forces it. That step often halves the file size with little visible change.

Step 5: Convert and Preview the Result

Click the Convert button. Each file converts independently, so you can watch progress card by card.

Each card shows the new JPG size next to the old WebP size. Expect the JPG to be larger, since WebP is more efficient than JPG.

Click Compare on a card to inspect the result side by side. Drag the split handle across the image and zoom into text, edges, and the matte fill.

If the text looks soft, raise the quality by five points and convert again. The original WebP stays in the queue, so you never re-upload.

Step 6: Download the JPG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one JPG. The filename keeps your original name with a .jpg extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted JPG inside. That is far quicker than downloading them one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a JPG in your photo viewer, confirm it looks right, and check that it opens in the app that needs it.

Keep the original WebP files as well. A JPG can never become a perfect WebP again, so the originals are your safety net.

Converting a Batch of Downloads

Select many WebP files at once in the file picker. They all load into the queue, and one click converts them all to JPG.

Use the same matte colour and quality for the whole batch. Consistency matters when the images belong to the same document or project.

Watch for error badges as the batch runs. A single corrupted file shows a warning without stopping the rest, so the good files still finish.

Download the batch as a ZIP and unzip it into a fresh folder. Mixing converted JPGs with the original WebP files in one folder causes confusion later.

Getting a Pure White Background

For a logo that needs a white background, set the matte colour to pure white, hex code ffffff. That fills every transparent area with clean white.

Check the result against a white page. The logo should blend seamlessly, with no visible edge where the transparency used to be.

If the logo has soft shadows, those shadows flatten onto the white. That is expected, since JPG cannot store partial transparency, and the result still looks clean.

For a logo that must work on many backgrounds, do not convert to JPG at all. Keep it as WebP or PNG, where the transparency survives.

Fixing Common Problems

If a transparent sticker gets a black box, the matte colour was black or unset. Change it to white or your page colour and convert again.

If the text looks blurry, the quality was too low. Raise the slider to 90 percent and convert again from the original WebP.

If a file fails to convert, check that it is a real WebP. A file without an extension may be a JPG or PNG in disguise, and the tool reads the contents to decide.

If an animated WebP comes out as a still image, that is expected. The converter draws only the first frame, since JPG cannot store animation.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick WebP files from your downloads or gallery. The conversion runs on the phone, and the JPGs download to your device.

Keep phone batches small, since large WebP files take time to decode on a mobile processor. Ten files at a time is comfortable.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large batch of downloads, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make many files far easier to manage.

Converting Downloaded Wallpapers

Wallpapers downloaded from websites are often WebP files, and they need converting before they can be used as a desktop background on an older system. A JPG wallpaper works everywhere.

Convert the wallpaper to JPG at 90 percent quality. The image fills the screen, the colours stay rich, and the file opens as a background on any operating system.

Check the dimensions before converting. A wallpaper should match your screen resolution, and a small image stretched across a large screen looks blurry regardless of the format.

For a phone wallpaper, convert at the phone's screen size. A 1080 by 1920 JPG is sharp on most phones, and the file is small enough to set quickly.

Keep the WebP original if the wallpaper matters. It is the best-quality copy, and you can convert it again if you switch to a device with a different screen size.

Converting WebP for a School Project

School projects often need images from the web, and those images are usually WebP. A school computer with old software may not display them, so convert to JPG first.

Convert the images to JPG at 85 percent quality before adding them to the project. The JPGs embed cleanly in PowerPoint, Word, and Google Slides, and the project opens on any school computer.

For a project with many images, convert the whole set at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the JPGs are ready to drop into the slides.

Check the images after converting. A quick look at each one confirms it displays correctly, so the project has no broken images on presentation day.

Cite the image sources in the project. A converted image still belongs to its creator, and a proper credit shows good academic practice.

Converting WebP for a CV

A CV photo must open on the recruiter's computer, and a WebP photo may fail. Convert it to JPG before adding it to the CV, and check the result carefully.

Use a professional headshot, not a casual snapshot. The photo should show your face clearly against a plain background, with good lighting and a neutral expression.

Convert the photo to JPG at 90 percent quality. The face stays sharp, the colours stay natural, and the file looks professional to the recruiter.

Check the file size after converting. A CV with a 4MB photo may exceed the portal's upload limit, so compress the image if the total file is too large.

Keep the CV as a PDF after adding the photo. A PDF preserves the layout on every device, while a Word document may rearrange itself on a different computer.

Converting WebP for a Printer

Printers expect JPG or PNG, and a WebP file may be rejected by the print shop's system. Convert to JPG before sending the file, and check the shop's requirements first.

Keep the quality at 90 percent or higher for prints. A photo printed at 6 by 4 inches needs every pixel of detail, and a low-quality JPG shows its flaws on paper.

Check the dimensions before printing. A web image at 800 pixels wide prints well at 3 inches but poorly at 10 inches, so match the print size to the pixel count.

For a large print, use the highest-resolution source you have. A WebP downloaded from a website is often small, and no conversion can add the pixels a large print needs.

Order a test print for an important photo. A single small print confirms the colours and sharpness before you commit to a large order.

Converting WebP Received by Email

Images sometimes arrive by email as WebP attachments, and they refuse to open in the recipient's photo viewer. Converting them to JPG fixes the problem immediately.

Save the attachment to your device first, then drop it into the converter. Converting straight from the email app rarely works, since the file must exist on your device.

Check the image quality after saving. Email services sometimes compress attachments, so the saved file may already be lower quality than the original.

After converting, reply with the JPG attached if others need it. A JPG opens for everyone in the thread, while the original WebP fails for half of them.

Keep the original attachment in your email as a backup. If the conversion goes wrong, you can download it again and try different settings without asking the sender to resend it.

Converting WebP for a Family Album

A family album needs photos that open on every device, and WebP may fail on an older relative's phone. Converting the album to JPG first means everyone sees the same photos.

Convert the whole album in one batch at 85 percent quality. The photos look clean, the files open everywhere, and the album is ready to share with the whole family.

Sort the photos first, removing blurry shots and duplicates. A family album of three hundred photos often has fifty worth keeping, and converting only those saves real time.

Share the JPGs through a shared album or a cloud link. Everyone sees the same photos, regardless of their device or its age, and nobody needs to install anything.

Keep the WebP originals in an archive folder after sharing. They are the best-quality copies, and you can convert them again if a different relative needs a different format.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get a pure white background behind a converted logo?

Set the matte colour to pure white, hex code ffffff, before converting. That fills every transparent area with clean white, so the logo blends seamlessly onto white pages. Check the result against a white background before using it, since soft shadows flatten onto the white during conversion.

Can I convert WebP files that have no file extension?

Yes. Some websites save images without an extension, but the tool reads the actual file contents rather than trusting the name. Drop the file in, and the decoder recognises it as WebP. If it is really a JPG or PNG in disguise, the tool handles that too.

What quality setting should I use for WebP screenshots?

85 percent or higher is best for screenshots, since small text blurs quickly at lower quality. For photos, 85 percent is also the sweet spot, balancing size and quality well. Drop to 80 percent only when a file size limit forces it.

Can I convert many WebP downloads to JPG at once?

Yes. Select many WebP files at once in the file picker, and one click converts them all. Each file converts independently, and you download the whole batch as a single ZIP. Use the same matte colour and quality for the whole batch to keep results consistent.

Will the converted JPG open in Word and PowerPoint?

Yes. JPG is natively supported by Microsoft Word, PowerPoint, Photoshop, and every other office and design app. The converted JPG is a standard file with no special requirements, so it opens and prints exactly like a camera JPG.

Does WebP to JPG conversion work offline?

Once the page has loaded, the conversion runs locally in your browser and does not need the internet. You need a connection only to open the page the first time. After that, you can convert WebP files offline until you close the tab.
