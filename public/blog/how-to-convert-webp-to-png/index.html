How to Convert WebP to PNG Losslessly

Convert WebP images to PNG in a few steps. Upload, convert, check transparency, and download — one file or a whole batch.

A WebP image from a website often needs to become a PNG before you can edit it, print it, or open it in an older program. Converting it takes seconds, and the result is a perfect copy.

There is no quality slider to worry about, since PNG is lossless. Upload, convert, and download, and the pixels are preserved exactly.

This guide walks through each step, with the checks that confirm the conversion worked.

Before You Start: Know Why You Are Converting

Ask yourself what the PNG is for. Editing, printing, and compatibility with older software are good reasons. Making the file smaller is not, since PNG is larger than WebP.

Check that PNG is actually required. Many modern tools accept WebP directly, and converting to PNG only adds size without improving the image.

Find the best WebP original you have. The PNG will preserve it exactly, flaws included, so start from the highest-quality copy.

Files up to 25MB each are accepted. That covers high-resolution downloads and large screenshots without any preparation.

Step 1: Open the WebP to PNG Tool

Open the WebP to PNG tool in your browser. The page works on Windows, Mac, Linux, ChromeOS, Android, and iOS.

You will see an upload area and a convert button. There is no quality slider, since PNG is lossless and there is no quality level to choose.

No account is needed, and nothing is uploaded. The conversion happens on your device, which matters for personal images and downloaded files.

If you are on a phone, the page works in your mobile browser. Tap the upload area to pick WebP files from your downloads or gallery.

Step 2: Upload Your WebP Files

Drag your WebP files onto the upload area, or click it to open the file picker. You can select many files at once for a batch.

Each file appears as a card with a thumbnail, its dimensions, and its size. A quick scan confirms the right files loaded and nothing extra slipped in.

Some websites save images without a file extension. Those files still convert, since the tool reads the actual contents rather than trusting the name.

You can also paste a copied image with Ctrl+V on a computer. That is handy when the WebP is in your clipboard from another app.

Step 3: Convert the Files

Click the Convert button. The tool decodes each WebP, draws it onto a canvas, and encodes it as a PNG.

Conversion is fast, usually a second or two per file. Larger files take a little longer, since there are more pixels to encode losslessly.

Each card updates with the new PNG size next to the old WebP size. Expect the PNG to be much larger, often five to ten times the WebP size for photos.

If a file fails, read the error message. A corrupted WebP or a misnamed file is the usual cause, and the rest of the batch keeps converting.

Step 4: Confirm Transparency Survived

Click Compare on a logo card to see the WebP and the PNG side by side. Zoom into the edges and confirm the transparency looks clean.

Look for halos or fringes around the logo. A clean edge means the alpha channel copied exactly, which is what you want.

Check soft shadows too. A drop shadow should fade gradually, not in visible bands, which would mean something went wrong.

If anything looks different, re-upload the original WebP and convert again. The conversion should be exact, so a visible difference means a problem.

Step 5: Download the PNG Files

Click Download on a single card to save one PNG. The filename keeps your original name with a .png extension.

For a batch, click Download All to get a single ZIP with every converted PNG inside. That is far quicker than downloading them one by one.

Check the downloaded files once before you use them. Open a PNG in your photo viewer and confirm it looks right and opens in the app that needs it.

Keep the original WebP files as well. A PNG can never become a perfect WebP again, so the originals are your safety net.

  2. Click Convert and wait for each card to finish.
  3. Confirm transparency survived with Compare.
  4. Download single PNGs or the whole batch as a ZIP.

Converting a Batch of WebP Images

Select many WebP files at once in the file picker. They all load into the queue, and one click converts them all to PNG.

Watch the total size as the batch runs. A batch of WebP photos can become a very large ZIP as PNGs, so check that the destination can handle it.

Use the Compare viewer on one or two files before downloading the batch. It confirms the transparency survived and the colours match.

Download the batch as a ZIP and unzip it into a fresh folder. Mixing converted PNGs with the original WebP files in one folder causes confusion later.

Using the PNG for Editing

Open the PNG in your editor of choice. Photoshop, Figma, Canva, and PowerPoint all accept PNG natively, and the lossless format survives repeated saves.

Add your text, annotations, or overlays to the PNG version. Each save keeps the image exactly as sharp as the last, with no accumulating damage.

Export the finished result in the format the destination needs. A web page wants WebP or JPG, while a print shop may want the PNG itself.

Keep the PNG master after exporting. If you need another export later, you can make it from the PNG without reopening the original WebP.

Fixing Common Problems

If the PNG comes out much larger than expected, that is normal for photos. PNG stores every pixel exactly, and a detailed photo needs many bytes to do that.

If the colours look shifted, the source WebP may have a colour profile that did not survive. Compare the PNG against the WebP carefully, and keep the WebP if the shift matters.

If a file fails to convert, check that it is a real WebP. A file without an extension may be a JPG or PNG in disguise, and the tool reads the contents to decide.

If an animated WebP comes out as a still image, that is expected. The converter draws only the first frame, since PNG cannot store animation.

Tips for Phones and Tablets

On a phone, tap the upload area and pick WebP files from your downloads or gallery. The conversion runs on the phone, and the PNGs download to your device.

Keep phone batches small, since large PNGs take time to encode on a mobile processor. Five files at a time is comfortable.

Downloads land in your phone's Downloads folder. On iPhone, check the Files app; on Android, check your file manager.

For a large batch, use a desktop. The faster processor and bigger screen make many files far easier to manage.

Converting WebP for Photoshop

Photoshop supports WebP now, but many plugins and batch actions still expect PNG. Converting to PNG before importing avoids the surprises that break a workflow.

Convert the WebP files to PNG, then open them in Photoshop. The lossless format handles layers, masks, and adjustments without degrading with each save.

For a batch of images, convert them all first, then open the PNGs together. That order keeps the Photoshop session smooth, instead of stopping to convert each file mid-project.

Export the finished work as WebP or JPG for the web. The PNG is the working format, while the export format depends on where the image will live.

Keep the PNG masters in the project folder after exporting. If the client asks for changes, the masters are ready, and the exports are just delivery copies.

Converting WebP for PowerPoint

Older versions of PowerPoint do not display WebP images, so a slide with a WebP photo may show a broken icon on the presentation computer. Converting to PNG first removes the risk.

Convert the WebP images to PNG before adding them to the deck. The PNGs embed cleanly in every PowerPoint version, and the slides open correctly anywhere.

For a deck with many images, convert the whole set at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the PNGs are ready to drop into the slides.

Check the total deck size after converting. A presentation over 50MB may struggle to email or upload, so compress the images if the file is too large.

Test the deck on the presentation computer before the talk. A slide that looks fine on a laptop may show a broken image on an older machine, and a quick test catches it.

Converting WebP for a Print Shop

Print shops expect JPG or PNG, and a WebP file may be rejected by their system. Convert to PNG before sending the file, and check the shop's requirements first.

Keep the full resolution when converting for print. A web image at 800 pixels wide prints well at 3 inches but poorly at 10 inches, so match the print size to the pixel count.

Check the colours after converting, since WebP and PNG handle colour profiles slightly differently. A quick comparison confirms the print colours match the screen colours.

For a printed document with text, PNG is the right target. The lossless format keeps every letter edge crisp on paper, which matters for contracts and forms.

Order a test print for an important job. A single small print confirms the colours and sharpness before you commit to a large order.

Converting WebP for a Form

Upload forms list JPG and PNG as accepted formats, and a WebP file bounces straight back. Convert to PNG before uploading, and check the form rules first.

For a form that demands a maximum file size, PNG may be too large. A PNG photo can be several megabytes, while the same image as JPG is a fraction of that size.

If the PNG is too large, convert the WebP to JPG instead. The WebP to JPG converter produces a smaller file that still opens everywhere.

Check the dimensions as well as the format. A form that demands a specific pixel size will reject an image that is the right format but the wrong shape.

Upload the converted image early, before the deadline. A last-minute conversion leaves no time to fix a problem, while an early upload leaves room to try again.

Converting WebP Received in a Chat

Images often arrive in chats as WebP files, and they refuse to open on your computer. A friend sends a photo from a website, and your photo viewer shows a broken icon.

Save the image to your device first, then drop it into the converter. Converting straight from the chat app rarely works, since the file must exist on your device.

Check the image quality after saving. Chat apps often compress images automatically, so the saved file may already be lower quality than the original.

After converting, share the PNG back to the chat if others need it. A PNG opens for everyone in the group, while the original WebP fails for half of them.

Keep the original message as a backup. If the conversion goes wrong, you can download the image again and try different settings without asking the sender to resend it.

Converting a Batch for a Project

A project often needs a whole set of WebP images converted to PNG at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the PNGs are ready to drop into the design tool.

Sort the images by purpose before converting. Logos, photos, and screenshots may need different handling afterwards, and sorting first keeps the project organised.

Use the Compare viewer on one or two files before downloading the batch. It confirms the transparency survived and the colours match the originals.

Download the batch as a ZIP and unzip it into the project folder. A fresh folder keeps the converted PNGs separate from the WebP originals.

Keep the WebP originals in an archive folder after converting. They are the best-quality copies for the web, and you can convert them again if the project needs a different format later.

Converting WebP for a School Project

School projects often need images from the web, and those images are usually WebP. A school computer with old software may not display them, so convert to PNG first.

Convert the images to PNG before adding them to the project. The PNGs embed cleanly in PowerPoint, Word, and Google Slides, and the project opens on any school computer.

For a project with many images, convert the whole set at once. A batch conversion takes minutes, and the PNGs are ready to drop into the slides.

Check the images after converting. A quick look at each one confirms it displays correctly, so the project has no broken images on presentation day.

Cite the image sources in the project. A converted image still belongs to its creator, and a proper credit shows good academic practice.

Frequently asked questions

How do I confirm transparency survived the conversion?

Click Compare on a logo card to see the WebP and PNG side by side. Zoom into the edges and look for halos or fringes, which would mean a problem. Check soft shadows too, since they should fade gradually. A clean edge means the alpha channel copied exactly.

Can I convert WebP screenshots to PNG without quality loss?

Yes. The conversion copies every pixel exactly, so a WebP screenshot becomes a PNG with the same pixels and the same sharp text. The PNG is larger, but the image is identical. For screenshots you plan to edit, that exactness is worth the extra size.

How large can a single WebP file be for conversion?

Each WebP file can be up to 25MB, which covers high-resolution downloads and large screenshots. Because conversion runs on your device, large files process at local speed with no upload waiting time.

Can a WebP file without an extension be converted?

Yes. Some websites save images without a file extension, but the tool reads the actual contents rather than trusting the name. Drop the file in, and the decoder recognises it as WebP. If it is really a JPG or PNG, the tool handles that too.

Does WebP to PNG work on phones?

Yes. Open the tool in your phone browser, pick WebP files from your downloads or gallery, and download the PNGs. The conversion runs on the phone, and the files download to your device. Keep phone batches small, since large PNGs take time to encode on mobile.

When should I pick WebP to PNG over WebP to JPG?

Choose PNG when you need to keep transparency or plan to edit the image many times, since PNG is lossless and opens in every editor. Choose JPG when the destination demands it or when file size matters more, since JPG is far smaller for photos.
