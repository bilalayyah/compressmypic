How to Remove a Background from an Image

Remove the background from any photo in six steps: pick the right photo, upload it, check the cutout with Split Compare, and download a transparent PNG.

Removing a background from a photo takes less than a minute once you know the steps. Pick a good photo, upload it, check the cutout, and download it.

This guide walks through the whole process on the CompressMyPic Background Remover, from the first model load to the finished file. It also covers the problems that show up most often and how to fix them.

No design skill is needed. If you can drag a file into a browser window, you can remove a background.

Your photo never leaves your device. The whole process runs in your browser, so private photos stay private from start to finish.

Before You Start: Pick the Right Photo

A good result starts before you upload anything. The photo you choose decides most of the quality, so spend a minute picking the right one.

Choose a photo where the subject is clear and fills a good part of the frame. A person, product, or pet that stands out from the background gives the AI an easy job.

Prefer a simple background. A plain wall, a table, or an open sky separates cleanly, while a crowded room or a patterned backdrop makes the AI guess.

Check the light. Soft, even light on the subject and a little contrast with the background helps the model find the outline. Harsh shadows behind the subject cause trouble later.

If the photo is wider than it needs to be, crop it first. The Image Cropper trims the extra scene, and a tighter frame usually produces a cleaner cutout.

Step 1: Wait for the AI Model to Load

Open the Background Remover page. On your first visit, the page downloads a compact AI model of about 35 MB before the tool is ready.

The download takes a few seconds on a fast connection and longer on slow Wi-Fi. Wait for the page to say the model is ready before you upload a photo.

This download happens only once. The model is cached in your browser, so later visits start instantly with no waiting at all.

If you are on mobile data, use Wi-Fi for the first visit. The 35 MB model download is the only large transfer, and everything after it is small.

Step 2: Upload Your Photo

Drag your photo into the dropzone, or click it to pick a file from your device. The tool accepts JPG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, BMP, and AVIF files up to 20 MB.

Upload one photo at a time. The tool processes a single photo so the AI model can focus all of your device memory on one clean cutout.

HEIC photos from iPhones work directly. The tool converts them to a decodable image automatically, so you do not need to convert them first.

Large photos take a little longer because the AI runs on your device. A 20 MB camera shot needs more processing time than a small phone photo, so be patient for a few seconds.

Keep the original file safe. The tool never changes your source photo, so you can always upload it again or use it for a different edit later.

Step 3: Let the AI Cut Out the Subject

Once the photo is uploaded, the AI scans it and builds a mask that marks each pixel as subject or background. This usually takes a few seconds.

You do not need to draw anything or mark the subject yourself. The model has learned what people, products, and animals look like, so it finds the outline on its own.

When it finishes, the page shows your original photo next to the transparent cutout. The subject stays untouched, and the background becomes empty space.

If the first pass catches a shadow as part of the subject, use Re-run Removal. The model is deterministic, so the result is usually identical, but a changed input can improve it.

Step 4: Check the Cutout with Split Compare

Before you download, check the cutout carefully. Click Split Compare to drag across the before-and-after view and see exactly what was kept and what was removed.

Look at the edges first. Hair, fur, ears, handles, straps, and thin parts are where mistakes show up, so zoom in and inspect them closely.

Watch for leftover background around corners and edges. A small patch of the old background can survive near a complex outline, and it is easy to miss at a small size.

Also check for missing pieces. A same-colour sleeve, a glass part, or a thin cord can be cut away with the background, leaving a gap in the subject.

If the result is not clean, do not force it. Try a photo with stronger contrast, crop closer to the subject, or re-shoot with a plainer background, then upload again.

Pay special attention to hands, feet, and anything the subject holds. Small items near the edge of the frame are easy to cut by mistake, and the compare view shows them clearly.

Step 5: Choose Transparent or a Solid Background

When the cutout looks good, choose how to export it. Transparent keeps the background empty, so the cutout works on any colour or design behind it.

Pick transparent when the cutout will go into a design tool. Canva, Photoshop, Figma, PowerPoint, and Google Slides all accept transparent PNGs, and the cutout blends into any slide or layout.

Pick white when the image is for a marketplace listing. Amazon and eBay prefer crisp white-background images for main product thumbnails, and the white export matches that rule.

Pick black for a dramatic look, or a custom colour to match your brand. The custom option takes any hex colour, so the background can match your website or packaging exactly.

Remember that a transparent PNG is larger than a JPG of the same image. PNG stores every pixel losslessly, so a big detailed cutout can outweigh the original photo.

Step 6: Download Your Cutout

Click the download button for the version you chose. The transparent PNG is named after your photo with _no_bg added, so you can find it easily later.

The download keeps the original resolution. A 4000-pixel product photo stays 4000 pixels wide, so the cutout is sharp enough for print as well as the web.

After downloading, click New Image to process the next photo. There is no daily limit, so you can work through a whole set of photos one by one.

Save the transparent PNG as your master copy. From it, you can create white-background, resized, or compressed versions later without removing the background again.

Fixing Common Problems

A faint halo around the subject is the most common flaw. It happens when the edge between subject and background is soft, and the model keeps a thin ring of the old background.

A halo is easier to prevent than to fix. Re-shoot with a plain background that contrasts with the subject, and the edge will come out cleaner.

If part of the subject is missing, the subject probably blends into the background. Same-colour clothing, glass, or camouflage patterns confuse the model, so change the backdrop and try again.

If the background is busy, the edges may look rough. Crop the photo closer to the subject before uploading, which removes distractions and gives the model less to guess about.

If the cutout looks fine at a small size but rough when zoomed in, the photo itself may be low resolution. Start from the highest-quality original you have, not a compressed copy.

If a shadow survives as a dark patch under the subject, it was probably strong in the original photo. Re-shoot with softer, more even light, and the shadow will usually disappear from the cutout.

Removing Backgrounds from Product Photos, Step by Step

Product photos follow the same steps, with a few extra checks. A listing image is judged at a glance, so the edges and the background colour matter more than anywhere else.

Photograph the product on a plain surface in soft daylight before you start. A white sheet, a plain table, or a light wall gives the AI a simple background to remove.

Upload the photo and check the cutout at full size. Zoom into corners, handles, straps, and cords, where leftover background hides most often.

Export on white for the main listing image. Marketplaces expect a pure white background, and the white export from the tool matches that requirement directly.

Keep the transparent PNG as well. The same cutout can go on a coloured banner, a social post, or your own store page, where a white box would look out of place.

Work through a batch one photo at a time. There is no daily limit, so you can remove the background from a whole product range in one sitting.

Removing Backgrounds from Portraits

Portraits need extra care around hair. Loose strands, curls, and flyaway hairs blur the edge between the person and the background, and a compact model may soften them.

Ask the person to stand against a plain wall or curtain. A clear contrast line around the head and shoulders gives the model the easiest outline to follow.

Light the face well and keep the background a little darker if you can. A well-lit subject against a darker backdrop separates more cleanly than a dim subject in a bright room.

After removal, use Split Compare and look closely at the hairline, the ears, and the shoulders. A small error there is easy to spot at full size and hard to notice later.

Place the finished portrait on a background that suits its purpose. A neutral grey or soft colour works for a professional profile, while a bold colour suits a social thumbnail.

Removing a Background on Your Phone

The tool works on mobile browsers too. It runs in Safari on iOS and Chrome on Android, with the same upload, compare, and download controls.

Use Wi-Fi for the first visit so the 35 MB model downloads quickly. After that, the model is cached and the removal itself runs on the phone.

Large photos take longer on a phone because the AI runs on the phone processor. A high-resolution camera shot may need a few extra seconds compared with a laptop.

Downloading works the same way. The PNG saves to your photos or files app, ready to share or open in a design app on the phone.

What to Do Next with Your Cutout

A cutout is rarely the final image. Most people place it on a new background, resize it, or compress it before publishing, and each step has a tool for it.

The Add Background to Image tool puts your cutout on a colour, a gradient, or another photo. It is the natural next step after removal.

The Image Cropper trims the cutout to the exact shape you need, such as a square for a profile picture or a wide crop for a banner.

The Image Converter changes the format. A transparent PNG can become a WebP for faster web pages, or a white-background version can become a JPG for a listing.

If the PNG is too large for your page, compress it afterwards. The main compressor on the CompressMyPic homepage shrinks images while keeping them sharp.

Store the transparent cutout in a folder with your other design assets. The same file can serve a listing, a social post, and a slide deck, so you only remove the background once.

Frequently asked questions

Should I crop my photo before removing the background?

Yes, if the scene is busy. Cropping closer to the subject removes distractions and gives the AI less background to confuse with the subject, which usually produces a cleaner edge. Use the Image Cropper first, then upload the cropped photo. If the photo already has a plain background, cropping is optional.

Can I change the background colour after I download the cutout?

Only if you downloaded the transparent PNG. A transparent cutout can be placed on any colour later, in any design app or with the Add Background to Image tool. If you downloaded the white-background version, the white is baked into the file and cannot be removed, so always keep the transparent PNG as your master copy.

Why does my cutout have a faint halo around the edges?

A halo is a thin ring of the old background that survives because the edge between subject and background is soft. It happens most with blurry edges, low contrast, or busy scenery. Prevent it by re-shooting with a plain background that contrasts with the subject — a dark subject on a light wall, for example — and the edge will come out much cleaner.

Can I remove the background from a logo that sits on a white background?

Yes, and logos are one of the easiest cases because their outlines are sharp and the background is plain. Upload the logo, check the cutout, and download the transparent PNG. The result sits neatly on a website header, a business card, or a slide without a white box around it.

How do I add a new background after removing the old one?

Download the transparent PNG first, then open the Add Background to Image tool and upload the cutout. You can place it on a solid colour, a gradient, or another photo, and export the finished image. Doing removal and background replacement as two separate steps keeps the cutout reusable for other designs.

What size should my photo be for the best result?

Use the highest-quality original you have, up to the 20 MB limit. A sharp, high-resolution photo gives the model a clear edge to follow, while a small or heavily compressed copy blurs the outline and produces a rougher cutout. The tool keeps the original resolution in the download, so starting big costs nothing.
